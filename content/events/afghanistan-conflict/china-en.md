China has approached the Afghanistan conflict under the banner of "non-interference in internal affairs," consistently evaluating NATO and U.S. military operations critically. The official Chinese narrative holds that the U.S.-led "War on Terror" deepened instability in Afghanistan rather than bringing about stability.

After the Taliban's seizure of power in 2021, China became one of the few countries to maintain embassy functions and has actively cultivated pragmatic relations with Taliban leadership. China's primary concerns are threefold: first, border security with Xinjiang (suppressing the activity of the East Turkestan Islamic Movement); second, the possibility of **BRI (Belt and Road Initiative) extension** into Afghanistan; and third, mineral resource development rights, including the Mes Aynak copper mine.

At the same time, China has clearly demanded that the Taliban prohibit support and shelter for Uyghur armed groups. On the subject of human rights, China avoids comment under the stance of "respecting each country's right to choose a development path suited to its national conditions," and criticizes Western values-based diplomacy as a "double standard."
