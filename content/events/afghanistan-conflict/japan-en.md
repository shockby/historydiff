Japan has consistently maintained a stance of "non-military peace contribution," refraining entirely from military involvement in Afghanistan. Since 2001, through contributions to JICA and international organizations, Japan has provided over **700 billion yen** in development and humanitarian assistance covering agriculture, infrastructure, education, and healthcare.

Following the Taliban's seizure of power in August 2021, Japan conducted evacuation operations for Japanese nationals and Afghan collaborators from Kabul, but the rapid deterioration of conditions prevented large-scale evacuations. This experience sparked domestic debate in Japan about the role of the Self-Defense Forces in protecting Japanese nationals abroad.

Japan's current position is to maintain engagement through continued humanitarian assistance, while not formally recognizing the Taliban government and conditioning dialogue on the protection of women's rights to education and employment. Support channeled through NGOs and UN agencies is the primary mechanism, and Afghanistan is positioned in Japanese policy as a symbolic case for "human security" diplomacy.

The Japanese government has maintained its embassy operations in Kabul and engaged directly with Taliban representatives through venues such as the June 2024 Third UN Doha Meeting, while joining G7 partners in expressing grave concern over the August 2024 morality law restricting women's rights. Japan also supported the 2024 initiative led by Australia, Canada, Germany, and the Netherlands to hold Afghanistan accountable under the UN Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), balancing human rights pressure with humanitarian aid.
