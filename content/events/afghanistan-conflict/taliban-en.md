The Taliban (the "Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan") frames its capture of Kabul in August 2021 as "liberation from 20 years of foreign occupation." The official narrative holds that U.S.-led intervention merely sustained a corrupt puppet regime, and that the will of the Afghan people is the restoration of governance based on Islamic law (Sharia).

While the Taliban government seeks international recognition, it has explicitly stated it has no intention of changing domestic policies, including the prohibition on women's secondary and higher education, employment restrictions, and strict dress codes in public life. These policies are justified as "protecting Islamic values and culture," and Western criticism is dismissed as "cultural imperialism."

To cope with economic isolation, the Taliban prioritizes building relations with China, Russia, Pakistan, and Central Asian states. The return of approximately **$7 billion in frozen Afghan central bank assets** is the paramount diplomatic challenge, and the government aims to build a self-sufficient economic base through agriculture and mining. It has designated ISIS-Khorasan (ISKP) as a threat to its own sovereignty and has engaged in limited intelligence sharing with the United States.

Between 2024 and 2025, the Taliban administration ('Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan') simultaneously codified strict Islamic governance at home and achieved diplomatic breakthroughs with regional powers. Domestically, it promulgated a **35-article 'Propagation of Virtue and Prevention of Vice' law in August 2024**, defending severe restrictions on women's public appearance and voices as sovereign Sharia implementation against UN criticism. Diplomatically, following the acceptance of Taliban-appointed ambassadors by China and the UAE in 2024, **Russia's Supreme Court removed the Taliban from its terrorist list in 2025 and Moscow granted formal diplomatic recognition**, which Kabul hailed as a historic triumph breaking Western isolation.
