In response to the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, the United States launched Operation Enduring Freedom to dismantle al-Qaeda and topple the Taliban regime that harbored them. With the support of NATO allies, the military operation rapidly overthrew the Taliban government, but the subsequent nation-building effort encountered far greater difficulties than anticipated.

After approximately **20 years** of military presence and expenditure of over **$2 trillion**, President Biden ordered the full withdrawal of U.S. forces in August 2021, just before Kabul fell to the Taliban. The chaotic nature of the withdrawal, combined with the rapid collapse of the Afghan National Army, drew international criticism and sparked debates about U.S. diplomatic credibility.

The current U.S. government does not recognize the Taliban government and continues to freeze approximately **$7 billion** in Afghan assets. Through "over-the-horizon" counterterrorism operations, the U.S. maintains surveillance and strike capabilities against groups such as ISIS-Khorasan (ISKP) via drones and intelligence agencies. Regarding the frozen assets, the U.S. is engaging in discussions with the Taliban on the conditions of severance of ties with terrorist organizations, protection of women's rights, and counternarcotics efforts.
