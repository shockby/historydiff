In Armenia the destruction of Ottoman Armenians between 1915 and 1923 by the Young Turk government is regarded as genocide and is a cornerstone of national memory. Armenians call it **Metz Yeghern ("the Great Crime")**. The starting point is **24 April 1915**, when Armenian intellectuals and leaders were arrested in Constantinople, and that date is the annual day of remembrance. Men were killed, women and children were driven on death marches toward the Syrian desert (Deir ez-Zor), property was confiscated and orphans were forcibly assimilated. The death toll is put at about **1.5 million**.

Armenian textbooks and officials cite the Ottoman courts-martial of 1919–20, reports by German and American diplomats, Raphael Lemkin's reference to the Armenian case when conceiving "genocide," and the 1997 resolution of the International Association of Genocide Scholars. They describe Turkish denial as a "continuation of the crime." International recognition remains a foreign-policy pillar. Yet the **2009 Zurich Protocols** were never ratified, and the border has been closed since 1993. After the loss of Nagorno-Karabakh in 2023, Armenian society debates whether to pursue normalization with Turkey or to prioritize recognition demands.
