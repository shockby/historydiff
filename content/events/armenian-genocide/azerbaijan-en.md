Azerbaijan's ties with Turkey are summed up by the slogan "**one nation, two states**," and Baku strongly backs Ankara's rejection of the genocide designation. The government does not accept the 1915 events as genocide under international law and criticizes the recognition campaign as politically motivated. Because the campaign is seen as weakening Azerbaijan's position over Nagorno-Karabakh, President Aliyev has repeatedly voiced solidarity with Turkey.

Azerbaijani historiography instead emphasizes suffering inflicted on Azerbaijanis, notably the **March 1918 Baku massacres** and the **1992 Khojaly massacre**, and seeks international recognition of these as a counter-narrative to Armenian claims. Since Azerbaijan took full control of Karabakh through the 2020 and 2023 military operations, historical disputes have continued alongside border delimitation and peace treaty talks. Dissenting views from independent scholars and civil society are limited by constraints on the domestic speech environment.
