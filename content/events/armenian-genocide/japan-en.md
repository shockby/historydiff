The Japanese government has not formally recognized the events as "genocide," and has generally taken the position that Turkey and Armenia should address the historical issue through dialogue between themselves. Japan has a tradition of friendship with Turkey dating back to the Ertuğrul incident of 1890, along with substantial economic and diplomatic interests, and is often described as cautious on recognition. The Diet has not passed any resolution on the matter.

Japanese high-school world history textbooks usually mention the "Armenian Question" and the 1915 deportations and mass killings briefly, but the level of detail and whether the word genocide is used vary by publisher. Japanese academia mostly introduces Western genocide-studies frameworks while placing the events in the context of nationalism and war in the late Ottoman Empire. Public interest is modest. The prevailing tone is that researchers should compare the claims of both Turkey and Armenia and verify them against primary sources.
