In Korean historiography, the 663 AD Battle of Baekgang (also known as the Battle of Baekchon River) represents the climactic and tragic denouement of the "Baekje Restoration Movement" (*Baekje Buheung Undong*) following the 660 AD fall of Baekje, as well as a watershed moment in Silla's ultimate unification of the Three Kingdoms.

In 660 AD, allied Silla forces led by King Muyeol (Kim Chunchu) and General Kim Yu-sin, alongside a 130,000-strong Tang army under Su Dingfang, captured Sabi Fortress. King Uija surrendered, marking the official demise of the kingdom. However, loyalist Baekje generals and provincial leaders refused to accept subjugation. Figures such as Heukchi Sangji, Gwisil Boksin, and the Buddhist monk Dochim organized widespread guerrilla resistance. Based in fortresses like Imjon and Juryu, the restoration forces reclaimed more than 200 cities, besieging the Tang garrison in Sabi. To solidify political legitimacy, they repatriated Prince Buyeo Pung from Yamato (Japan) and formally requested substantial military reinforcements from their insular ally.

Tragically for the restoration cause, fatal internal discord erupted at the leadership level. Factional rivalry led Gwisil Boksin to assassinate Dochim; subsequently, Prince Buyeo Pung, suspecting Boksin of treason, executed Boksin. This fratricidal infighting fatally compromised military cohesion, command authority, and troop morale right as allied enemy armies converged.

King Munmu of Silla and General Kim Yu-sin seized the strategic initiative, joining forces with Tang generals Liu Rengui and Sun Renshi to execute a joint land-sea encirclement of Juryu Fortress and the Baekgang estuary. In August 663 AD, the allied Silla and Tang forces confronted the approaching Yamato armada supporting the restoration troops. Utilizing superior maritime formations and incendiary weapons, the allied fleet trapped and incinerated over 400 Yamato vessels.

The cataclysmic defeat at Baekgang brought an end to three years of fierce resistance. Juryu and Imjon fortresses capitulated shortly thereafter. Prince Buyeo Pung fled into exile in Goguryeo, while numerous Baekje aristocrats, artisans, and refugees migrated en masse to Japan. In modern Korean history education, this battle is understood as a pivotal turning point: while lamenting the tragedy of external great-power intervention crushing Baekje's sovereignty, it cleared the way for the subjection of Goguryeo (668 AD) and the subsequent Silla-Tang War, ultimately securing an autonomous unified Korean civilization south of the Taedong River.
