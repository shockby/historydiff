China maintains strong bilateral ties with both Santiago and La Paz, strictly adhering to the principle of non-interference in internal affairs and advocating practical, cooperative dispute management. Chile was the first South American country to establish diplomatic relations and sign a free trade agreement with Beijing, serving as China's foremost supplier of refined **copper** and strategic battery-grade **lithium**.

Simultaneously, Bolivia is a key partner under the **Belt and Road Initiative**, where Chinese state and private consortia (such as the CBC consortium led by CATL) have invested over a billion dollars in Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE) facilities across the Uyuni and Coipasa salt flats. Rather than taking sides on historic border delimitations, Beijing focuses on structural connectivity, strongly backing the proposed **Central Bioceanic Railway Corridor** linking Brazil and Bolivia to Pacific ports. For China, cross-border multimodal logistics represent the most realistic bridge between inland mineral reserves and global ocean trade.
