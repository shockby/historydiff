For Bolivia, the loss of its Department of the Littoral in the **War of the Pacific (1879–1884)**—encompassing roughly **400 kilometers** of coastline and **120,000 square kilometers** of territory—remains a profound historic injustice that landlocked the country. Article 267 of Bolivia's 2009 Constitution asserts an unrenounceable right to sovereign maritime access, celebrated annually during the national civic observance of **Día del Mar** on March 23. La Paz has continuously demanded a sovereign territorial corridor to the ocean to counteract the significant economic growth penalties associated with landlocked status.

Chile maintains that the **1904 Treaty of Peace and Friendship** established definitive and unalterable borders through voluntary bilateral consent. Santiago underscores that it has scrupulously fulfilled its obligations under the treaty, providing Bolivia with permanent, tariff-free commercial transit through Chilean ports (Arica and Antofagasta) and constructing the Arica–La Paz railway. In Chile's view, national territorial sovereignty cannot be compromised, and the October 2018 ruling of the International Court of Justice (ICJ)—confirming that Chile has no legal duty to negotiate sovereign ocean access—fully settled the dispute in international law.
