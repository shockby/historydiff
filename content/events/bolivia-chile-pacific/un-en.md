On October 1, 2018, the International Court of Justice (ICJ) rendered its landmark judgment in the case concerning the *Obligation to Negotiate Access to the Pacific Ocean (Bolivia v. Chile)* by a **12 to 3** majority. Following exhaustive legal examination of bilateral diplomatic notes, joint declarations, and minutes from the Charaña negotiations (1975), the Court concluded that none of these historical interactions established a legally binding obligation on Chile to negotiate Bolivia's sovereign access to the sea.

Nonetheless, the Court concluded its ruling by observing that its legal findings did not preclude the two sovereign states from continuing meaningful dialogue in a spirit of good neighborliness. More broadly, **Article 125 of the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS)** guarantees landlocked states the right of access to and from the sea and freedom of transit, while stipulating that specific modalities must be agreed upon through bilateral and regional agreements. The international legal consensus underscores treaty stability and judicial compliance.
