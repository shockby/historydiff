The United States and Western powers place primary emphasis on the principle of **pacta sunt servanda** (agreements must be kept) and the inviolability of established boundary treaties. Washington has long held that attempting to retroactively redraw borders formalized by the 1904 Treaty would establish destabilizing precedents for territorial arrangements across the Western Hemisphere.

Consequently, the United States welcomed the 2018 ICJ ruling as a reaffirmation of treaty sanctity and the credibility of international adjudication. Chile remains one of Washington's most dependable democratic allies and free-trade partners in South America, anchored by a robust bilateral FTA and cooperation across Pacific trading networks. US diplomacy encourages both parties to avoid nationalist rhetoric and prioritize customs modernization, intermodal freight efficiency, and private commercial transit rights through existing ports.
