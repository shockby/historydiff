China argues that responsibility should be measured by **historical cumulative emissions** and **per-capita emissions**. It treats the UNFCCC's (1992) principle of "**common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities (CBDR-RC)**" as the foundation of climate talks, insisting that developed countries, having industrialised first, must **lead on cuts and provide finance and technology to developing countries**. At the same time China pledged in 2020 to **peak CO2 before 2030 and reach carbon neutrality before 2060**, and in 2025 announced a target to cut net emissions **7–10% from their peak by 2035**. It is the world's largest installer of renewable energy.

Yet China now emits roughly **30%** of global CO2 each year, more than all developed countries combined. Western governments argue China should no longer be treated as a typical developing country and criticise continued coal plant approvals. Beijing replies that part of its emissions come from manufacturing goods for rich markets, and that its per-capita and cumulative emissions remain below America's. At COP26 (2021) it backed, with India, the last-minute softening of coal "phase-out" to "phase-down".
