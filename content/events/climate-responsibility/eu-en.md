The EU's Climate Law makes **climate neutrality by 2050** legally binding, with a **55% cut** below 1990 levels by 2030. In negotiations it argues that holding warming to 1.5°C requires **all major emitters** to peak early and reach net-zero CO2 around mid-century, and it presses large emerging economies such as China for more ambition. At COP29 (2024) parties agreed a new finance goal led by developed countries (**$300 billion a year by 2035**); the EU pushed to broaden the contributor base so that wealthier emerging economies also contribute, at least voluntarily.

The EU began phasing in its **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** in 2023, with full application from 2026. Brussels presents it as protection against competitiveness loss and "carbon leakage"; India, China, Brazil and South Africa call it "**a unilateral trade measure that violates the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities**". The EU maintains that it is compatible with WTO rules.
