India stresses that its **per-capita emissions are less than half the global average** (about 2 tonnes versus roughly 14 in the US) and that it accounts for only around **3%** of historical cumulative emissions. It argues that "**the countries that emitted heavily in the past must bear the heavier burden, and developing countries must not be denied their right to develop**". Fossil fuels, including coal, remain essential for poverty reduction and electricity access, and India says rich nations have already consumed the carbon budget. At COP26 (2021) it pledged **net-zero by 2070** and **500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030**, while securing the change from coal "phase-out" to "phase-down".

At COP28 India insisted that **all fossil fuels**, not coal alone, be treated equally, and at COP29 it called the $300 billion finance goal "**a paltry sum**". Framing its stance as **climate justice**, it sees Western demands as double standards. Yet India is the world's third-largest emitter and on the front line of heatwaves, floods and severe air pollution, so it also faces domestic tension between a rapid solar build-out and continued fossil fuel dependence.
