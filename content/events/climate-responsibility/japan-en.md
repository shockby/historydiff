Japan has emphasised a **fair and effective framework in which all major emitters participate**. Because the US had withdrawn and China and India had no binding targets, Japan **declined to join Kyoto's second commitment period (from 2013)**. Under the Paris Agreement it targets net-zero by 2050 and a **46% cut by FY2030** from FY2013 levels, and in 2025 adopted new targets of **60% by FY2035** and **73% by FY2040**. It supports developing countries through public and private climate finance and technology transfer via the bilateral **Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM)**.

Japan accepts the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities yet leans toward asking large emerging economies such as China and India to shoulder commensurate responsibility. At the same time, its **continued reliance on coal power** has drawn criticism from international NGOs, and it has received the "Fossil of the Day" award at multiple COPs. Through the **Asia Zero Emission Community (AZEC)** concept, using ammonia and hydrogen co-firing and CCS, Japan promotes "multiple pathways" tailored to each country's circumstances.
