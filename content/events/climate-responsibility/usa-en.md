The United States has long argued that **all major emitters** must cut emissions. In 1997 the Senate voted **95–0** on a resolution opposing any climate treaty that exempted developing countries, and the Kyoto Protocol was never ratified. Washington welcomed the Paris Agreement's structure, in which every party submits its own pledge (NDC), but **President Trump announced withdrawal in 2017**; President Biden rejoined in 2021 and pledged a **50–52% cut** below 2005 levels by 2030. The second Trump administration, in office from 2025, again moved to withdraw and prioritises expanded fossil fuel production.

The US accounts for roughly **a quarter** of historical cumulative CO2 emissions and still has some of the highest per-capita emissions. Because China's annual emissions now far exceed America's, many US voices argue that unilateral American cuts are futile. Developing countries and NGOs counter that rich nations have been slow to deliver promised climate finance ($100 billion a year) and that policy swings with each US election undermine trust.
