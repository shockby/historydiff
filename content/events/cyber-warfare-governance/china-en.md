The Chinese government places the principle of "**cyber sovereignty** (*wangluo zhuquan*)" and the building of a "**Community with a Shared Future in Cyberspace**" at the center of international digital governance, arguing that the UN Charter principles of sovereign equality and non-interference in internal affairs apply squarely to cyberspace. Beijing maintains that every state has the sovereign right to regulate its domestic internet infrastructure and cross-border data flows in accordance with its own laws and national conditions (codified in the **2017 Cybersecurity Law** and **2021 Data Security Law**). From China's perspective, Western demands for an "unrestricted free flow of information" serve to entrench the monopoly of U.S. tech giants and facilitate foreign interference or "color revolutions." China also opposes the automatic application of Western-drafted law-of-war frameworks (such as the *Tallinn Manual*) to cyberspace, warning that invoking armed-attack thresholds in digital space **legitimizes the militarization of cyberspace and unilateral pre-emptive strikes**. Instead, Beijing advocates negotiating new UN-centered rules and launched its **Global Initiative on Data Security** in **2020**.

Regarding Western **public attributions** accusing Chinese state-backed groups (**APT1**, **Hafnium**, **Volt Typhoon**, **Salt Typhoon**, **MirrorFace**, etc.) of espionage and infrastructure infiltration, Beijing categorically rejects the charges, stating that "**China firmly opposes and legally combats all forms of cyberattacks and is itself one of the world's primary victims of cyber intrusions.**" Chinese officials argue that technical attribution in cyberspace is notoriously complex due to IP spoofing and proxy routing, and accuse the United States and its allies (Five Eyes, Japan, etc.) of fabricating politically motivated "smears" without sharing verifiable forensic evidence through international channels.

Since 2022, China's **National Computer Virus Emergency Response Center (CVERC)**, the Ministry of State Security (MSS), and Chinese cybersecurity firms have published a series of counter-attribution reports. Citing the **2013 Edward Snowden (PRISM) revelations** and the CIA's **Vault 7** leaks—including the "Marble Framework" designed to obfuscate source code and plant false-flag linguistic markers—Beijing characterizes the United States as the world's true "**Empire of Hacking and Global Surveillance**." Specifically, CVERC reports accused the NSA's Tailored Access Operations (**TAO**) of infiltrating China's **Northwestern Polytechnical University** (published in 2022), the Wuhan Earthquake Monitoring Center, and Huawei headquarters, and in **2024** released multiple investigative reports alleging that "**Volt Typhoon**" was a disinformation narrative concocted by U.S. intelligence agencies and contractors to frame cybercriminal ransomware activity as Chinese state sabotage in order to secure congressional funding and contain China.
