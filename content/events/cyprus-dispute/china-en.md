China has maintained a consistent stance of respecting the UN framework on the Cyprus dispute and has never recognized the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (TRNC). This aligns with China's general principle that unilateral declarations of independence within recognized states are impermissible under international law — a position directly relevant to its own sensitivities regarding Taiwan, Tibet, and Xinjiang. Recognizing the TRNC would create an uncomfortable precedent that could be cited in support of independence movements China considers threats to its own territorial integrity, making recognition politically inconceivable from Beijing's perspective. The TRNC is recognized only by Turkey, and China maintains alignment with the overwhelming international consensus.

At the same time, China has cultivated substantial economic and investment ties with the Republic of Cyprus. The port of Limassol holds strategic significance as a potential node in the Belt and Road Initiative for Eastern Mediterranean connectivity. Chinese investors and high-net-worth individuals heavily utilized Cyprus's investor visa ("golden passport") program as a pathway to European market access, before the program was terminated in 2020 under EU pressure amid concerns about money laundering risks. Despite that program's closure, bilateral economic ties between China and Cyprus remain significant, with Chinese firms maintaining investments in Cypriot real estate, finance, and infrastructure.

On the question of reunification negotiations, China has expressed rhetorical support for a UN-facilitated settlement without actively positioning itself as a mediator. Beijing maintains "constructive ambiguity" — endorsing the UN process in general terms while avoiding commitments that could complicate relations with either NATO-member Turkey or EU-member Greece and Cyprus. In the context of intensifying geopolitical competition between China and the West since 2022, Cyprus's role as a potential conduit for circumventing Western sanctions has attracted additional attention, embedding the island's unresolved political status within a broader strategic conversation about Eastern Mediterranean connectivity and financial flows.
