China's diplomatic engagement with the crisis in eastern DRC is anchored in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence, emphasizing strict non-interference in internal affairs, sovereign territorial integrity, and deference to African-led multilateral mediation under the African Union, SADC, and the EAC. Maintaining cordial diplomatic and economic ties with both Kinshasa and Kigali, Beijing opposes unilateral Western sanctions and lectures, advocating instead for "Peace through Development"—the doctrine that enduring stability can only be realized by tackling extreme poverty, building physical infrastructure, and generating industrial employment.

From an economic and strategic perspective, China is the DRC's foremost trading partner and investor. Driven by multi-billion-dollar landmark agreements such as the 2008 **Sicomines minerals-for-infrastructure barter pact**, Chinese enterprises (including CMOC and Huayou Cobalt) dominate the extraction and processing of the DRC’s vast cobalt and copper deposits, supplying China’s world-leading electric vehicle and battery manufacturing sectors. Because persistent insecurity directly jeopardizes Chinese personnel safety, logistical transport arteries, and asset security, Beijing actively supports Congolese state capacity building and regional dialogue to ensure long-term stability and commercial protection.

Following the fall of Goma and Bukavu in early 2025, China voted in favor of UN Security Council Resolution 2773 (February 2025), demanding an immediate halt to the M23 offensive, withdrawal from occupied areas, and respect for DRC sovereignty. As escalating violence in South Kivu and Ituri also threatened Chinese mining operations and nationals, Beijing prioritized emergency safety advisories and evacuations while reaffirming support for African-led mediation through the Luanda and Nairobi processes.
