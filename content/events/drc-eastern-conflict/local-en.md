For the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), the three decades of unending bloodshed across its eastern Kivu provinces constitute brazen territorial aggression and illicit resource plundering orchestrated by neighboring Rwanda. The Kinshasa government, citing exhaustive UN investigative findings, asserts that the **M23 rebel movement** operates as a proxy for the Rwanda Defence Force (RDF), equipped with advanced weaponry, air defense systems, and direct Rwandan troop reinforcements. Kinshasa contends that Kigali’s primary strategic objective is the systematic annexation and smuggling of the region's immense mineral wealth—notably **coltan** (tantalum), gold, and tin vital for global electronics—and insists on the unconditional withdrawal of Rwandan forces as the non-negotiable prerequisite for peace.

Conversely, the Rwandan government led by President Paul Kagame officially refutes direct military involvement with the M23 while invoking the fundamental right to national self-defense against existential threats. Kigali’s primary security grievance centers on the **Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda (FDLR)**, an armed group founded by perpetrators of the 1994 Rwandan Genocide against the Tutsi. Rwanda contends that the Congolese national army (FARDC) actively collaborates with and integrates genocidal FDLR remnants, fueling systemic persecution, ethnic cleansing, and xenophobic violence against Congolese Tutsis and Banyamulenge communities. Kigali maintains that until Kinshasa completely eradicates the FDLR and ceases state-sponsored hate speech, Rwanda will take all necessary sovereign measures to protect its borders and vulnerable kinship populations.
