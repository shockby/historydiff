The United Nations and the international humanitarian architecture have maintained unwavering focus on both the flagrant breaches of national sovereignty and the catastrophic humanitarian catastrophe engulfing eastern Congo. Initially established in 1999 as MONUC and later renamed **MONUSCO**, the mission grew into one of the largest peacekeeping deployments in UN history. In 2013, the Security Council authorized the pioneering **Force Intervention Brigade (FIB)** with an explicit offensive mandate to neutralize armed rebellions. Nevertheless, the proliferation of over 120 insurgent factions and recurrent cross-border interference severely strained mission effectiveness, precipitating widespread public protests and culminating in Security Council Resolution 2717 (2023) authorizing an orderly, phased withdrawal beginning in 2024.

Reports by the UN Security Council Group of Experts have systematically documented evidence detailing Rwandan military deployments in direct support of the M23 alongside illicit operational alliances between Congolese military units and the genocidal FDLR militia. Simultaneously, UN agencies—including UNHCR, IOM, and OHCHR—sound the alarm over unprecedented human suffering: over **7 million internally displaced persons (IDPs)**, chronic famine, and pervasive **systemic sexual violence** utilized as a calculated weapon of warfare. The UN reiterates that lasting stability requires honoring the territorial integrity of the DRC, completely severing external proxy support, and fully implementing regional mediation frameworks.
