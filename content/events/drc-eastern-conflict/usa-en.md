The United States maintains a firm policy upholding the territorial integrity and sovereignty of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, explicitly calling upon Rwanda to halt all military and logistical backing to the M23 rebels and withdraw its forces from Congolese soil. Aligning with UN investigative conclusions, Washington has designated key M23 commanders and external facilitators under US sanctions while deploying senior diplomatic mediators to reinforce the African-led Luanda and Nairobi peace frameworks to broker enduring ceasefires.

A pillar of US engagement was established by **Section 1502 of the 2010 Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act**, which instituted groundbreaking statutory disclosure mandates requiring publicly traded corporations to audit supply chains for conflict minerals (tantalum, tin, tungsten, gold) originating from the DRC and adjoining countries. In recent years, heightened geopolitical competition over clean energy transition inputs—particularly cobalt and lithium—has intensified US strategic interest in countering dominant Chinese mining concessions in the DRC, accelerating Western initiatives to build transparent, secure, and responsible critical mineral corridors free from armed coercion.

The United States strongly condemned the early 2025 capture of Goma and Bukavu by M23 and Rwandan Defense Force (RDF) units as a flagrant violation of DRC sovereignty, expanding targeted financial sanctions and visa restrictions against Rwandan military officials and M23 leaders. At the same time, Washington has pursued diplomatic engagement—supporting mediation via Luanda and Doha while exploring bilateral critical-mineral partnerships with Kinshasa—to secure transparent cobalt and tantalum supply chains and curb illicit conflict-mineral flows.
