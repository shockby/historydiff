Algeria and advocates across former French colonies contend that Paris's refusal to issue an official state apology or pay reparations for **132 years of colonial rule in Algeria (1830–1962)**—including massive land dispossession, the **1945 Sétif, Guelma, and Kherrata massacres**, an independence war that Algeria commemorates as claiming **1.5 million martyrs** (while French historians estimate 300,000–500,000 Algerian deaths), and contamination from **17 French nuclear tests in the Sahara (1960–1966)**—leaves historical justice incomplete. Critics likewise point to the brutal suppression of the **1947 Malagasy Uprising** (tens of thousands killed), France's **1955–1971 “hidden war” in Cameroon** against the UPC, and decades of post-independence monetary and military tutelage via the **CFA franc** and **Françafrique** interventions as the root causes behind the popular and governmental demands across Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad, and Senegal that forced French troops out between 2022 and 2025.

In **New Caledonia (Kanaky)**, the indigenous **Kanak** pro-independence coalition (**FLNKS**) maintains that the **December 2021 third referendum** lacked legitimacy because Paris refused to postpone the vote during the COVID-19 pandemic and traditional Kanak mourning rites, triggering an independence boycott (turnout fell to 43.9%). When Paris moved in **May 2024** to unfreeze provincial voting rolls for recent metropolitan arrivals, Kanak movements denounced the measure as “recolonization through demographic dilution.” Following unrest that left **14 dead** and over €2 billion in damages—and the controversial transfer of Kanak leader Christian Tein to pre-trial detention in mainland France until June 2025—the FLNKS rejected the **July 2025 Bougival Accord** as falling short of genuine decolonization and full sovereignty.

Within metropolitan France, civil rights groups and residents of suburban **banlieues** argue that colorblind “republican universalism” and increasingly restrictive interpretations of *laïcité* mask structural discrimination, spatial segregation, and racial profiling by police targeting citizens of North and Sub-Saharan African descent. Activists draw a direct line from colonial policing—symbolized by the **October 17, 1961 Paris massacre**, when Paris police under Prefect Maurice Papon killed between several dozen and up to 200 peaceful Algerian demonstrators—to contemporary police shootings such as the June 2023 killing of Nahel Merzouk.
