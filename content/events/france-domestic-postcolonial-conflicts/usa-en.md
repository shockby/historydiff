The **United Nations Special Committee on Decolonization (C-24)** lists both **New Caledonia** (re-inscribed in 1986) and **French Polynesia** (re-inscribed by the UN General Assembly in 2013 over strong French objections) as Non-Self-Governing Territories subject to international decolonization monitoring. During the **May 2024 unrest in New Caledonia**, **UN human rights experts**, the **Pacific Islands Forum (PIF)**, and the Melanesian Spearhead Group (MSG) voiced concern over the dilution of indigenous **Kanak** voting power and dispatched a PIF troika mission to Nouméa. Regarding domestic policing in mainland France, following the June 2023 police shooting of Nahel Merzouk, the **UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR)** and the **UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination (CERD)** urged France to address “deep issues of racism and racial discrimination in law enforcement”—criticism the French Foreign Ministry rejected as “unfounded.”

For the **United States** and Western allies, France is an indispensable NATO ally and a resident Indo-Pacific power, yet Anglo-American policymakers and scholars have long viewed France's assimilationist model and post-colonial policies with ambivalence—dating back to U.S. critiques during the 1956 Suez Crisis and the Algerian War.

In contemporary geopolitics, the foremost concern for Washington and European capitals has been how entrenched anti-French resentment across the **Sahel** (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger) and West Africa precipitated the expulsion of French forces between **2022 and 2025** (and subsequently U.S. forces from Niger in 2024), creating a strategic vacuum rapidly filled by **Russia's Africa Corps (formerly the Wagner Group)** and expanding Chinese economic influence. Western strategic analysts frequently argue that decades of **Françafrique** patronage and military entanglement alienated West African publics and ultimately undermined broader Western counter-terrorism and diplomatic standing across the continent.
