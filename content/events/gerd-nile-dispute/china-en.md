China approaches the GERD dispute adhering strictly to the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence, emphasizing non-interference, respect for state sovereignty, and direct diplomatic consultation among riparian nations. Holding Comprehensive Strategic Partnerships with both Egypt and Ethiopia, as well as robust bilateral ties with Sudan, Beijing maintains a calibrated neutral posture, firmly supporting the mediation mandate of the African Union while opposing coercive Western-led sanctions or unilateral ultimatums at the UN Security Council.

On the ground, Chinese state-owned enterprises play a pivotal commercial role in the region's energy infrastructure. Chinese conglomerates, including State Grid Corporation of China, secured major contracts exceeding $1 billion to engineer and construct the high-voltage transmission networks, substations, and distribution grids conveying GERD's hydroelectric power to Ethiopian cities and industrial parks. Concurrently, China has invested tens of billions into Egypt's Suez Canal Economic Zone and New Administrative Capital. Prioritizing the overall stability of its **Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)** across the Red Sea and East Africa, Beijing urges constructive dialogue and shared developmental dividends.
