The Government of Japan approaches the GERD dispute from the perspective of maintaining longstanding diplomatic friendship, strategic partnerships, and substantial developmental cooperation with Egypt, Ethiopia, and Sudan alike. During bilateral summits and ministerial consultations with leaders from Cairo and Addis Ababa, Tokyo has consistently advocated for restraint, the avoidance of escalatory unilateral actions, and the negotiated conclusion of an amicable, trilateral agreement grounded in international law and mutual benefit.

Pragmatically, Japan contributes technical expertise and targeted official development assistance (ODA) through the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) to address underlying resource vulnerabilities across the Nile basin. In downstream Egypt, Japan finances cutting-edge water conservation infrastructure, wastewater reclamation, and high-efficiency agricultural irrigation systems. In upstream Ethiopia, Japan supports renewable energy grid integration, watershed afforestation, and capacity building. Promoting its trademark "Quality Infrastructure Investment," Japan advocates for cooperative regional water diplomacy that enhances climate resilience for all basin populations.
