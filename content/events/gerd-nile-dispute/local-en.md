For Ethiopia, the **Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD)** represents an inviolable sovereign right to socio-economic development, aimed at eradicating extreme poverty and electrifying the lives of over 60 million citizens lacking power. Contributing roughly 85% of the Nile's overall flow via the Blue Nile, Addis Ababa categorically rejects the 1929 and 1959 colonial-era treaties—which allocated the river's entire discharge to Egypt and Sudan—as illegitimate relics that historically disenfranchised upstream nations. Ethiopia maintains that non-consumptive hydroelectric power generation does not deplete water volume, thereby causing no significant harm to downstream neighbors while providing clean renewable energy to the entire East African region.

For Egypt, which relies upon the Nile for over 90% of its municipal, agricultural, and industrial freshwater needs, the GERD is perceived as an acute, existential threat to national survival. Cairo vociferously condemns Ethiopia’s unilateral impoundment of the reservoir without a prior comprehensive legal pact, arguing it sets a dangerous precedent violating customary international water law. Egypt’s core diplomatic demand is a legally binding treaty guaranteeing baseline volumetric water releases during prolonged multi-year droughts to prevent the catastrophic depletion of Lake Nasser behind the Aswan High Dam.

Sudan occupies a strategically nuanced middle position. On one hand, GERD offers immense hydrological advantages: regulating the Blue Nile's volatile seasonal flow, eliminating devastating floods, reducing sediment siltation, and supplying affordable imported electricity. On the other hand, because Sudan's Roseires Dam is located a mere 100 kilometers downstream with limited holding capacity, Khartoum stresses that absent real-time data sharing and operational coordination, its own critical hydraulic infrastructure and the safety of millions of Sudanese citizens along the riverbanks face unacceptable hazards, necessitating a binding trilateral governance framework.
