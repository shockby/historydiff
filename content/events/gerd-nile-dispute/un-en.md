The United Nations and the multilateral diplomatic community have treated the GERD controversy as a critical regional peace and security concern spanning the Arab and Sub-Saharan African spheres. Convening dedicated debates under Chapter VI of the UN Charter following submissions by Egypt and Sudan, the UN Security Council adopted Presidential Statement S/PRST/2021/18 in September 2021. The Council underscored that transboundary river controversies should be resolved peacefully, encouraging the three riparian capitals to avoid inflammatory unilateral declarations and resume structured negotiations under the auspices of the **African Union (AU)** to finalize a mutually acceptable pact.

International legal bodies and environmental agencies, such as UNEP, analyze the dispute through the dual tenets of the 1997 **UN Watercourses Convention**: the principle of "equitable and reasonable utilization" balanced against the "obligation not to cause significant harm." As global climate volatility exacerbates hydrological instability across the Horn and Nile basins, the UN advocates for institutionalized scientific transparency, real-time hydrological data exchange, collaborative drought management protocols, and basin-wide integration through platforms like the Nile Basin Initiative (NBI) to avert resource-driven geopolitics.

In September 2024, Egypt submitted a formal protest letter to the UN Security Council condemning Ethiopia's fifth GERD filling as a unilateral violation, while Ethiopia countered that its actions represented equitable utilization aligned with the **Nile Basin Cooperative Framework Agreement (CFA)**. Notably, following South Sudan's ratification, the **CFA officially entered into force on October 13, 2024** to establish a permanent Nile River Basin Commission, sharpening the legal divide as Egypt and Sudan rejected the treaty as non-binding on their historic water rights.
