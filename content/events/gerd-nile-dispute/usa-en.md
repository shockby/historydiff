The United States has engaged deeply in the GERD dispute, seeking to balance its historic strategic and security partnership with Egypt against the developmental imperatives of Ethiopia, an anchor state in the Horn of Africa. In late 2019 and early 2020, the Trump administration convened direct trilateral negotiations in Washington alongside the World Bank, drafting a compromise agreement governing filling phases and drought mitigation thresholds. However, when Ethiopia walked away from initialing the final draft—alleging the terms disproportionately favored Egyptian historical claims and infringed on sovereign prerogatives—the US temporarily paused certain security assistance to Addis Ababa, heightening friction.

The Biden administration recalibrated American posture, moving away from unilateral conditioning and reaffirming support for African Union-led mediation. Utilizing the US Special Envoy for the Horn of Africa, Washington emphasizes preventative diplomacy to avert any military escalation that could destabilize the vital Red Sea and Suez shipping corridors. The US continues to call for a legally binding, mutually acceptable accord on reservoir operations, emphasizing technical data transparency, regional grid connectivity, and peaceful compromise.
