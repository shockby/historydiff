For Guyana, Guayana Esequiba constitutes roughly **two-thirds** of its national territory—encompassing approximately **159,500 square kilometers**—and is home to more than 125,000 citizens. The Guyanese state and public firmly maintain that the **1899 Paris Arbitral Award** served as a "full, perfect, and final settlement" agreed upon by Great Britain and Venezuela. Following ExxonMobil's 2015 discovery of over **11 billion barrels** of recoverable oil in the offshore Stabroek block, Georgetown brought the matter to the International Court of Justice (ICJ), demanding absolute respect for established international treaties and borders.

Conversely, Venezuela across ideological spectrums considers the territory west of the Essequibo River an inalienable legacy inherited from the Spanish **Captaincy General of Venezuela (1777)**. In Venezuelan historiography, the 1899 arbitration was an imperialist fraud orchestrated through collusion between British and Russian judges. Caracas asserts that the **1966 Geneva Agreement** superseded the 1899 award, mandating bilateral negotiations for a mutually satisfactory resolution. The Maduro administration's December 2023 consultative referendum and subsequent annexation legislation have brought regional tensions to unprecedented heights.
