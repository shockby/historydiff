The United Nations and international legal bodies emphasize strict adherence to the prohibition of the threat or use of force (UN Charter Article 2(4)) and the peaceful settlement of disputes (Article 33). After decades of mediation under the Secretary-General's Good Offices process yielded no compromise, Secretary-General António Guterres determined in January 2018 that the means of settlement should be the **International Court of Justice (ICJ)**.

In December 2020, the ICJ affirmed its jurisdiction over the validity of the 1899 Arbitral Award. In April 2023, the Court rejected Venezuela's preliminary objection that the United Kingdom was an indispensable party. On December 1, 2023, the Court unanimously issued binding **provisional measures**, directing Venezuela to refrain from any actions that would alter the status quo prevailing in the territory administered by Guyana. The UN Security Council has convened repeatedly, urging both parties to respect judicial processes and maintain regional peace.
