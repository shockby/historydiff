During the Gulf War (1990–1991), China abstained on UN Security Council Resolution 678 authorizing the use of force. Beijing cited principles of non-interference in internal affairs while carefully avoiding a veto that could have damaged its relationship with Washington in the post-Tiananmen diplomatic environment.

China firmly opposed the 2003 Iraq War, aligning with France and Russia in the Security Council. Beijing condemned the U.S.-led invasion as unilateral action without UN authorization and a violation of Iraqi sovereignty. Chinese officials warned that such unilateralism threatened the international rules-based order. China also argued that the war's aftermath — sectarian chaos, the rise of extremism, and regional instability — vindicated its opposition. Both conflicts reinforced China's longstanding advocacy for multipolarity and non-interference, and deepened its wariness of U.S. hegemonic behavior. The Iraq War in particular became a key reference point in Chinese critiques of Western-led "humanitarian intervention" and regime change.
