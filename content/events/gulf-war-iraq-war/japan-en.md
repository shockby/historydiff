During the Gulf War (1990–1991), Japan contributed approximately \$13 billion to the multinational coalition but sent no personnel, drawing sharp criticism as "checkbook diplomacy." This episode was a watershed moment in Japanese security policy, directly leading to the enactment of the PKO Cooperation Act (1992).

In the Iraq War (2003), Prime Minister Koizumi supported the U.S. position and dispatched Self-Defense Forces to Samawah in southern Iraq — the first SDF deployment near an active conflict zone since World War II. The mission sparked intense domestic debate over the constitutionality of the operation and the definition of "non-combat zones." Japan also provided significant financial assistance for Iraq's reconstruction. The SDF withdrew by 2008. Both wars accelerated the deepening of the Japan-U.S. alliance and spurred reinterpretation of Japan's pacifist constitution, reshaping the trajectory of Japanese security policy for decades.
