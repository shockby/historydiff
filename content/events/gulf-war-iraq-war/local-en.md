During the Gulf War (1990–1991), Iraq's invasion of Kuwait was viewed by some in the region as a response to economic pressure from Kuwait's oil overproduction and unresolved debts from the Iran-Iraq War. The multinational coalition's bombing campaign devastated Iraq's infrastructure and caused widespread civilian casualties.

The Iraq War (2003–2011) saw a U.S.-led invasion topple Saddam Hussein's regime, yet no weapons of mass destruction were found. The subsequent collapse of security, sectarian violence, and insurgency inflicted prolonged suffering on Iraqi civilians. Opinions within Iraq remain divided: many condemn the invasion as unilateral imperialism, while others welcomed liberation from decades of brutal dictatorship. The post-war fragmentation of Iraqi society and the rise of extremist groups have left deep scars across the region.
