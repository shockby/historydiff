The Gulf War was authorized by UN Security Council Resolution 678 (1990), giving the multinational coalition a clear legal mandate to expel Iraq from Kuwait. The operation was widely seen as a textbook exercise of collective security under the UN Charter.

The 2003 Iraq War, by contrast, lacked explicit UN authorization. France, Germany, and Russia opposed the invasion, and UN weapons inspectors under UNMOVIC reported no confirmed WMD evidence. Secretary-General Kofi Annan later stated the war was "illegal" under the UN Charter, marking a profound fracture in multilateral governance. The subsequent failure to find WMDs vindicated the inspectors and fueled global skepticism of U.S. intelligence claims. While the UN played a role in post-war reconstruction, the episode seriously damaged the Security Council's credibility and the principle of multilateral decision-making in matters of international peace and security.
