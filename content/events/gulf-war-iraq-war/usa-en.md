The Gulf War (1990–1991), led by President George H.W. Bush with broad international support and UN authorization, is largely viewed in the U.S. as a successful exercise of multilateral force. Operation Desert Storm showcased precision weaponry and demonstrated overwhelming American military capability. It is widely remembered as a "just war" with a clear objective: liberating Kuwait.

The Iraq War (2003) under President George W. Bush remains far more contested. The administration's case for war rested on alleged WMDs and links to terrorism, both of which proved unfounded. While the swift military victory toppled Saddam Hussein, the subsequent occupation was marked by insurgency, Abu Ghraib, and sectarian bloodshed. The Chilcot and Senate Intelligence Committee reports later revealed intelligence failures and manipulation. American and British public opinion shifted sharply against the war, and its legacy continues to fuel debate about the legitimacy of preventive war and democracy promotion by force.
