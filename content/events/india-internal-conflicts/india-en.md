The Indian government frames domestic armed struggles as “**internal security threats**” and communal violence as matters of law and order, to be handled within the constitutional framework. In 2006 the Prime Minister called Maoist (Naxalite) violence the country's greatest internal security threat; the state has combined security operations with development and surrender schemes. The Home Ministry says affected districts fell from over **100** at the peak, set a target of eliminating Naxalism by **31 March 2026**, and has declared the goal met. In the Northeast it retains the **1958** Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act as necessary for security while bringing militants into politics through accords such as the Mizo Accord (1986) and the Bodo Accord (2020). It points to prosecutions through the courts and Special Investigation Teams for the 1984 anti-Sikh riots and the 2002 Gujarat riots, and says central intervention in Manipur since 2023 (President's Rule in 2025, a new state government in February 2026) restored order.
