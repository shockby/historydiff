The United States regards India as a key **strategic partner** against China and in economic terms, and has generally avoided formal criticism of its internal conflicts, treating them as domestic matters. Yet the State Department's annual human rights reports regularly cite extrajudicial killings under AFSPA, torture by security forces, violence against minorities and arbitrary detention. Since 2020 the U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF) has recommended designating India a “Country of Particular Concern”, but the State Department has not done so. In 2005 Washington revoked the diplomatic visa of then Gujarat Chief Minister Narendra Modi over the 2002 riots (relations normalized after he became Prime Minister in 2014). On the 2023 Manipur violence the U.S. voiced concern while saying it was for India to find a solution through dialogue. Balancing human rights against strategic interests defines the U.S. approach.
