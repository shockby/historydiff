China publicly proclaimed "strict neutrality" during the Iran-Iraq War while pragmatically selling arms to both belligerents. Beijing supplied Iran with F-7 fighters and missiles, and also provided weaponry to Iraq, profiting from the prolonged conflict and expanding its foothold in the defense export market.

For China, the war represented an opportunity to deepen access to developing-world markets while the US and USSR were each entangled with one side. The contradiction between China's espoused principle of "Third World solidarity" and its dual arms dealing drew criticism internationally.

With Iran specifically, China maintained practical economic and military cooperation despite ideological differences with the Islamic Republic, securing an enduring strategic foothold in the Middle East. Analysts view this war as an early template for China's pattern of leveraging regional conflicts to advance its arms industry and geopolitical influence.
