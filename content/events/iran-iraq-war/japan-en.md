For Japan, the Iran-Iraq War posed an acute energy security threat. Following the 1973 oil shock, Japan had deepened its dependence on Middle Eastern oil, with both Iran and Iraq serving as major suppliers. The risk of Strait of Hormuz closure loomed over the Japanese economy, and Tokyo watched the "Tanker War" with grave concern.

Diplomatically, Japan maintained strict neutrality, supporting neither belligerent. Japanese trading companies continued business with both countries, partly to preserve relationships for anticipated post-war reconstruction contracts. Tokyo's muted official criticism of Iraq's chemical weapons use drew domestic criticism for prioritizing economic interests over humanitarian principles.

The war accelerated Japan's efforts to strengthen strategic petroleum reserves and diversify its oil supply sources beyond the Middle East.
