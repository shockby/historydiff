## The Iraqi Perspective

Iraq justified its September 1980 invasion as a necessary move to reclaim sovereignty over the Shatt al-Arab waterway and to prevent the destabilizing export of Khomeini's Islamic Revolution to Iraq's Shia population. Saddam Hussein positioned himself as the defender of the Arab world. The use of chemical weapons was never officially acknowledged by Baghdad but was later confirmed by international investigators.

## The Iranian Perspective

For Iran, the conflict was the "Imposed War" (jang-e tahmili) — an unprovoked Iraqi aggression exploiting the chaos of the revolution. The Islamic Republic mobilized religious fervor to mount a counter-offensive aiming for Basra. The war, which claimed hundreds of thousands of lives, became a crucible legitimizing the revolutionary government. The Shatt al-Arab dispute remained unresolved after the 1988 ceasefire.
