The UN Security Council adopted Resolution 479 on September 28, 1980, calling for an immediate ceasefire, which both parties rejected. Cold War dynamics paralyzed the Council, with US and Soviet interests pulling in competing directions, preventing effective sanctions.

UN investigators confirmed Iraq's use of mustard gas and tabun nerve agent in 1984. The Security Council issued a condemnatory statement but imposed no binding penalties — an inaction widely criticized as enabling further chemical attacks, including those against Kurdish civilians at Halabja in 1988.

Resolution 598 (1987) was eventually accepted by both sides, and the ceasefire took effect in August 1988. The UN deployed UNIIMOG (UN Iran-Iraq Military Observer Group) to monitor compliance. The war exposed fundamental limits of UN collective security mechanisms during the Cold War era.
