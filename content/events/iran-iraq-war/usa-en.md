The Reagan administration viewed Iran's Islamic Revolution as the foremost threat to Middle Eastern stability, and backed Iraq on the logic that "the enemy of my enemy is a friend." Washington restored full diplomatic relations with Baghdad in 1984 and provided satellite intelligence, agricultural credits, and political cover as Saddam Hussein's forces used chemical weapons — support later confirmed by declassified documents.

The US Navy launched Operation Earnest Will (1987-88), escorting Kuwaiti tankers and effectively intervening on Iraq's side. The USS Vincennes' shootdown of Iran Air Flight 655 in 1988, killing 290 civilians, remains a defining symbol of the perilous entanglement.

Britain and France similarly continued arms exports to Iraq throughout the war. This pattern of Western "double standards" — condemning chemical weapons in principle while enabling their use in practice — continues to shape critiques of Western foreign policy credibility.
