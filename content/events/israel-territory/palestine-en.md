For the Palestinian people, 1948 is known as the "Nakba" (النكبة / the Catastrophe). That year, more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs were expelled or forced to flee from their ancestral lands, and over 500 villages were destroyed.

In Palestinian education and official positions, Palestine is a land where Arab people lived as an overwhelming majority since the Ottoman era. The 1947 UN General Assembly Resolution 181 (the Partition Plan) was an unjust decision that allocated 56% of the land to a Jewish state without the consent of the Palestinian people. At the time, Jewish people constituted only about 33% of Palestine's total population and owned only about 7% of the land.

The 1967 Six-Day War resulted in Israel occupying all of the West Bank, Gaza Strip, and East Jerusalem. UN Security Council Resolution 242 demanded Israeli withdrawal from the occupied territories, but Israel has not complied. The continued construction of settlements (approximately 700,000 Israeli settlers in the West Bank as of 2024) has severely restricted Palestinian access to land, water, and freedom of movement.

The 1993 Oslo Accords were supposed to pave the way for Palestinian statehood, but negotiations ultimately collapsed. Following Hamas's attack on October 7, 2023, and Israel's subsequent military operations in Gaza, UN agencies reported more than 45,000 Palestinian deaths by end of 2024, including civilians. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) is examining the applicability of the Genocide Convention to the situation. The Palestinian people assert that the "right of return" (حق العودة) for 1948 refugees and their descendants to their homeland, and the establishment of an independent state with East Jerusalem as its capital, are essential to achieving justice.
