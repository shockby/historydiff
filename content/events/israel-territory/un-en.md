The United Nations has been deeply involved in this issue since the 1947 Partition Plan (General Assembly Resolution 181). However, UN resolutions and recommendations are political products, and there is a difference in legal character between Security Council resolutions and General Assembly resolutions. The following is a summary of key international positions.

**Key UN Security Council Resolutions (potentially legally binding):**
- **Resolution 242 (1967)**: Demands "withdrawal of Israeli armed forces from territories occupied" and "acknowledgement of the sovereignty, territorial integrity, and political independence of every State." The interpretation of "withdrawal from territories" (whether "all" or "some" occupied territory) remains contested to this day.
- **Resolution 338 (1973)**: Demands a ceasefire after the Yom Kippur War and implementation of Resolution 242.
- **Resolution 2334 (2016)**: Explicitly states that Israeli settlement construction in the West Bank and East Jerusalem constitutes a "flagrant violation of international law." The United States refrained from using its veto for the first time.

**UN General Assembly Resolutions (no legal binding force; political statements):**
- **Resolution 3236 (1974)**: Reaffirms the right of the Palestinian people to self-determination, independence, and return.
- **Resolution ES-10 Series (Emergency Special Sessions)**: Multiple resolutions demanding a humanitarian ceasefire in Gaza have been adopted since 2023, but their practical effect has been limited.

**International Court of Justice (ICJ) Rulings:**
- **2004 Advisory Opinion**: Ruled that construction of the separation wall in the West Bank violates international law. Israel denies its binding force.
- **2024 Provisional Measures Order** (South Africa v. Israel): The ICJ issued provisional measures ordering Israel to "prevent acts within the scope of the Genocide Convention" in Gaza. The merits case is ongoing.
- **2024 Advisory Opinion**: The ICJ advised that "Israel's presence in the Occupied Palestinian Territories is unlawful under international law" and that Israel has "an obligation to bring its unlawful presence to an end as rapidly as possible."

These represent one dimension of "international community consensus," but the United States and Israel contest many of these positions, and the interpretation and enforceability of UN resolutions remain politically disputed.
