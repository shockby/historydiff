India maintains that the entire state of Jammu and Kashmir (J&K) is an integral and inseparable part of the Indian Union. This position is grounded in the Instrument of Accession signed on October 26, 1947, by Maharaja Hari Singh, the then-ruler of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir. India asserts that this accession was a fully legal act under international law, and that Pakistan's subsequent military intervention constituted a clear act of aggression.

In October 1947, when Pakistani-backed tribal militias invaded Kashmir, Maharaja Hari Singh requested Indian military assistance and signed the Instrument of Accession in exchange. Indian troops were immediately deployed and repelled the invaders, but a UN-brokered ceasefire eventually established the current "Line of Control" (LoC) in January 1949. India maintains that the plebiscite envisaged under UN Security Council resolutions need not be conducted because Pakistan has never fulfilled the precondition of first withdrawing its forces.

For India, the cornerstone of its position is the 1972 Simla Agreement, a bilateral treaty under which India insists that the Kashmir issue must be resolved exclusively through direct bilateral negotiations between India and Pakistan, without the involvement of any third party. India consistently rejects all attempts to "internationalize" the dispute, maintaining that bilateral dialogue is the only legitimate path to resolution.

In August 2019, the Indian government revoked Article 370 of the Indian Constitution, which had granted special autonomous status to Jammu and Kashmir, and reorganized the state into two Union Territories (Jammu and Kashmir, and Ladakh). The Indian government frames this as an essential step toward the state's "full integration" and "accelerated development." Pakistan and some Indian critics condemned the move as unconstitutional, but the Indian government maintains it was a legitimate administrative reorganization.
