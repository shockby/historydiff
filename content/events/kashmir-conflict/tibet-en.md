The Central Tibetan Administration (CTA, also known as the Tibetan Government in Exile, based in Dharamsala, India) views the Kashmir conflict not simply as a bilateral dispute between India and Pakistan, but within the broader context of Chinese expansionism in the region.

From the Tibetan perspective, Aksai Chin was historically a region where trade routes and traditional boundary zones connecting Tibet intersected, and before China's annexation of Tibet in 1950, it fell within the cultural and political sphere of influence of the Tibetan kingdom. Following China's takeover of Tibet, these areas came under direct Chinese administration, and the CTA frames China's growing control over Tibet, East Turkestan (Xinjiang), and the areas surrounding Kashmir as part of what it characterizes as a series of imperial conquests.

Tibetan leaders view China's investment in CPEC (China-Pakistan Economic Corridor) and its involvement in the Kashmir issue as strategic moves to secure access to the Indian Ocean and Central Asia. For the CTA, India is the country that has hosted the largest number of Tibetan refugees, and supporting India's territorial claims indirectly aligns with the cause of Tibetan liberation. As a result, the CTA's official statements tend to align with India's sovereign position.

In the CTA's view, a genuine resolution to the Kashmir conflict cannot be separated from the broader question of China abandoning its expansionism into the regions it calls "core interests," and the recognition of the genuine right to self-determination of the peoples of Tibet, East Turkestan, and Southern Mongolia. The CTA argues that peaceful resolution of all these interlinked disputes requires fundamental changes in China's approach to territorial claims.
