On April 21, 1948, the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 47, demanding a free and impartial plebiscite under UN supervision to determine the future of Kashmir. The resolution prescribed a sequenced process: first, Pakistan-backed tribal militias were to withdraw; then India was to progressively demilitarize; and finally a UN-supervised vote would be held. The resolution has never been implemented.

India's position is that the 1972 Simla Agreement transferred the Kashmir issue to a strictly bilateral framework, effectively rendering Resolution 47 moot. Pakistan counters that Security Council resolutions remain binding international law, and that a bilateral accord between two parties cannot extinguish the right of the Kashmiri people to self-determination enshrined in the resolution.

The UN has never formally revoked Resolution 47. The resulting gap — between a resolution that is "valid but unimplemented" on one reading and "superseded by bilateral agreement" on the other — remains the core legal fault line in the international dimension of the Kashmir dispute.
