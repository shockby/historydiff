China's response to NATO's 1999 bombing of Yugoslavia was one of fierce condemnation. From Beijing's perspective, the air campaign — conducted without explicit UN Security Council authorization — represented a blatant violation of state sovereignty and a textbook example of Western "hegemonism" disguised as humanitarian concern. The accidental NATO bombing of the Chinese Embassy in Belgrade on May 7, 1999, which killed three Chinese journalists and was explained by the U.S. as a tragic navigational error using outdated maps, ignited intense anti-American sentiment across China and became a defining moment in Chinese public memory of Western unilateralism. China joined Russia in blocking any UNSC resolution endorsing Kosovo independence.

Beijing has consistently and forcefully opposed Kosovo's declaration of independence in February 2008, characterizing it as "a grave violation of the principles of sovereignty and territorial integrity enshrined in the UN Charter." The Kosovo issue is inseparable from China's core concerns about Taiwan, Tibet, and Xinjiang. Should Kosovo's independence become universally recognized as a legitimate exercise of self-determination, it would furnish legal and moral arguments applicable to secessionist movements that Beijing views as existential threats to Chinese territorial integrity. Accordingly, China has maintained unbroken support for Serbia's territorial claims over Kosovo and has cultivated its "iron friendship" with Belgrade through growing economic and infrastructure investment under the Belt and Road Initiative framework.

The 2010 International Court of Justice advisory opinion — which concluded that Kosovo's declaration of independence did not violate international law — drew a measured but firm Chinese rebuttal. Beijing characterized the opinion as a narrow technical finding specific to Kosovo's unique circumstances, insisting it should not be read as endorsing a general principle that self-determination declarations are permissible. China has continued to withhold recognition and has signaled opposition to any EU-Serbia normalization framework that would require Serbia to grant de facto recognition to Kosovo as a condition of EU accession. China's substantial Balkan investment portfolio — concentrated in Serbia in energy and transport infrastructure — reinforces the alignment of China's strategic economic interests with its political support for Belgrade's position on Kosovo.
