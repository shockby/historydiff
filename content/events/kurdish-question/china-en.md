China's position on the Kurdish question is firmly grounded in the principles of state sovereignty and territorial integrity. Beijing consistently opposes Kurdish self-determination or independence movements, a stance closely aligned with its own domestic sensitivity around Xinjiang, Tibet, and Taiwan.

As a permanent member of the UN Security Council, China has opposed or abstained on resolutions that could be interpreted as legitimizing external intervention in the internal affairs of sovereign states. During Turkey's 2019 Operation Peace Spring, China declined to join Western condemnation, framing Turkey's action in part as a counter-terrorism operation and opposing any sanctions that would infringe on state sovereignty.

Economically, China maintains extensive partnerships with Turkey, Iraq, Syria, and Iran through Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) infrastructure and energy investments. Stability in these relationships takes priority over support for Kurdish political aspirations. China also maintains pragmatic energy ties with the Kurdistan Region of Iraq.

Chinese state media tends to portray PKK armed activities as terrorism while frequently framing Western support for Kurdish forces in Syria as an infringement on Syrian sovereignty and a tool of geopolitical manipulation — a narrative that reinforces Beijing's broader critique of U.S.-led interventionism in the Middle East.
