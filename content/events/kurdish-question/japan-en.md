Japan's approach to the Kurdish question is grounded in respect for the sovereignty of Turkey, Iraq, Iran, and Syria, while engaging on humanitarian grounds in areas such as human rights protection and refugee support. As a close partner of NATO ally Turkey, Japan has generally acknowledged Turkey's designation of the PKK as a terrorist organization.

Regarding the Kurdistan Region of Iraq (KRI), Japanese companies have maintained energy sector investments, and the Japanese Embassy in Iraq operates a branch office in Erbil, the KRI capital. Japan has also provided indirect support to Kurdish regions as part of broader Iraq reconstruction assistance.

A domestically salient issue is the situation of Kurdish refugees and asylum seekers residing in Japan, primarily from Turkey, concentrated in cities such as Kawaguchi and Warabi in Saitama Prefecture. Japan's refugee recognition rate remains one of the lowest among OECD countries, and many Kurdish applicants endure prolonged provisional release status, drawing criticism from human rights organizations.

The Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs frames the Kurdish question as a "complex regional issue involving Turkey, Iraq, and Syria," supporting political dialogue and stability while refraining from direct commentary on the internal affairs of the countries involved.
