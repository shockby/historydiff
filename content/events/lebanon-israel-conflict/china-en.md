China upholds principles of non-interference and dialogue in the Lebanon-Israel conflict, consistently supporting restoration of Palestinian people's legitimate rights and a two-state solution. In the UN Security Council, Beijing takes a critical stance toward Israeli military operations while condemning unilateral US veto use as an obstacle to international order.

Following the 2006 war, China expanded its UNIFIL participation, deploying engineering units of several hundred personnel — part of a broader diplomatic strategy to increase its Middle East presence. China does not designate Hezbollah as a terrorist organization and maintains engagement with diverse Lebanese factions. While sustaining economic and technological ties with Israel, Beijing prioritizes strengthening relations with Arab states and the Islamic world in a balancing diplomacy. The Lebanon-Israel conflict is partly framed within China's broader strategic competition with US hegemony in the Middle East.
