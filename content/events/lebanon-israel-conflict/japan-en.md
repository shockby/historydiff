Japan maintains diplomatic relations with both Lebanon and Israel, consistently supporting dialogue-based conflict resolution in line with its emphasis on Middle East stability. Japan has contributed civilian experts and defense ministry officials rather than Self-Defense Force troops to UNIFIL, playing a neutral role through UN peacekeeping contributions.

Middle East stability is vital to Japan's energy security given its dependence on oil imports, and Tokyo expresses strong concern over any escalation. The Japanese government supports full implementation of UN Security Council resolutions and has continuously provided humanitarian assistance to Lebanon. Notably, Japan does not independently designate Hezbollah as a terrorist organization, placing it in a slightly different diplomatic posture from the US and EU. Engagement with Middle East peace remains a pillar of Japanese foreign policy amid ongoing efforts to diversify energy sources.

The Japanese government expressed deep alarm over the sharp escalation in Lebanon in autumn 2024 and the resulting civilian casualties, preparing Self-Defense Force transport aircraft for potential evacuations while providing emergency humanitarian grant aid through UN agencies. As a G7 member, Japan welcomed the November 2024 ceasefire and the January 2025 election of President Joseph Aoun, consistently supporting the full implementation of UNSC Resolution 1701—including Lebanese Armed Forces deployment south of the Litani River and Israeli withdrawal—to ensure the safe return of displaced civilians on both sides.
