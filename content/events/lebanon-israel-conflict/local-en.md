For Lebanon, the conflict with Israel represents a sustained history of occupation and aggression dating to the 1948 Nakba. Israeli military invasions in 1978 and 1982 violated Lebanese sovereignty and caused massive civilian casualties in the south. The 2006 Second Lebanon War saw 33 days of airstrikes and ground operations killing over 1,200 Lebanese civilians and displacing one million people.

Shebaa Farms is Lebanese territory, and Israeli occupation constitutes a violation of international law. Hezbollah is widely regarded in southern Shia communities as a resistance movement against Israeli aggression, deeply embedded in local society. The Blue Line is merely a provisional withdrawal line, not a final border settlement. The international community has consistently failed to hold Israel accountable for repeated violations of UN Security Council resolutions, leaving Lebanese sovereignty unprotected.

Cross-border hostilities following the October 2023 Gaza war escalated dramatically in September 2024 with the mass detonation of pagers and walkie-talkies, the assassination of Hezbollah Secretary-General Hassan Nasrallah in Beirut, and an Israeli ground invasion of southern Lebanon that killed nearly 4,000 people and displaced over 1.2 million—surpassing the devastation of the 2006 war. Although a US-France-brokered ceasefire took effect in November 2024 and Army Commander Joseph Aoun was elected President in January 2025 after a two-year vacuum, Lebanon continues to condemn Israel's retention of border outposts and repeated airstrikes as grave violations of Lebanese sovereignty and the truce.
