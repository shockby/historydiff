China has consistently advocated the principle of **non-interference in internal affairs** and peaceful resolution through dialogue in the Middle East. Beijing characterizes U.S. military interventions in Iraq and Syria, along with unilateral sanctions against Iran, as "hegemonic actions," asserting that multilateral diplomacy through the UN Security Council must remain the foundation of the international order.

In **March 2023**, China brokered a landmark agreement between Saudi Arabia and Iran to restore diplomatic relations, signed in Beijing. The Xi Jinping administration widely celebrated this as a crowning achievement of China's "major-country diplomacy," and the international community broadly interpreted it as a challenge to the U.S.-led Middle East order. China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs declared that "China has the will and the ability to play a constructive role in bringing peace to the Middle East."

From an energy security standpoint, China is the world's largest importer of Middle Eastern oil, with crude passing through the **Strait of Hormuz** accounting for roughly 40% of China's total imports. Instability in the region directly threatens the Chinese economy, making regional stability a vital national interest. Under the **25-year Comprehensive Cooperation Agreement** signed with Iran in 2021, China purchases Iranian oil at discounted prices despite Western sanctions while advancing infrastructure investment and technology transfers.

On the Palestinian question, China has consistently supported the **two-state solution**, calling for the establishment of an independent Palestinian state based on the 1967 UN resolutions. After the Gaza conflict erupted in **October 2023**, China criticized Israel's military operations as "excessive use of force" and repeatedly demanded an immediate ceasefire and the opening of humanitarian corridors. When Israel struck Iranian nuclear facilities in **2025**, China's Foreign Ministry condemned the attack as "a blatant violation of international law and an irresponsible act that heightens the risk of nuclear proliferation in the region."

Under the **Belt and Road Initiative**, China has expanded investment in ports, railways, and energy infrastructure across the Middle East, deepening economic ties with Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iraq, and Egypt. Rather than forming military alliances, China pursues influence through economic interdependence—actively projecting the image of a "peaceful great power" distinct from the United States.
