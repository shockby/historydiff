Most countries of the Global South have maintained a stance of **non-alignment and strategic autonomy** in the Middle East, refusing to side with any single camp. Carrying forward the spirit of the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) formed during the Cold War, nations such as Turkey, India, Pakistan, Indonesia, South Africa, and Brazil have declined to automatically endorse U.S.-led sanctions against Iran or its military actions, consistently prioritizing independent diplomatic judgment over bloc loyalty.

The escalation of Middle Eastern conflicts strikes Global South countries directly in the form of **surging energy prices and food crises**. When tensions rise among oil-exporting nations, fuel costs spike for import-dependent developing countries; instability in maritime shipping routes also drives up the cost of grain and fertilizer imports. The compounding effects of the **2022** Russia-Ukraine war and Middle Eastern tensions severely worsened food insecurity across Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia—an experience that has led the Global South to place early conflict resolution at the top of its priorities.

**Turkey** pursues its own independent diplomatic course. Despite being a full NATO member, the Erdoğan government has maintained economic and diplomatic ties with Iran while explicitly voicing support for Palestine. In the Gaza conflict that erupted in **October 2023**, Turkey called Israel a "war criminal state," recalled its ambassador, and suspended trade with Israel—drawing a clear line from its Western alliance partners. Turkey positions itself as a bridge between NATO and the Middle East, emphasizing its role as a voice for the Muslim world.

**India** skillfully deploys **strategic ambiguity**. It continues to purchase discounted crude oil from Iran despite Western sanctions, while maintaining deep defense and technology cooperation with Israel. The Modi government, during the Gaza conflict, called for an immediate ceasefire while carefully refraining from direct condemnation of Israel. India actively brands itself internationally as "an independent diplomatic power beholden to no great power."

Many Global South countries sharply criticize what they call **Western double standards**. Iran, which does not possess nuclear weapons, faces crippling international sanctions, while Israel—a country widely believed to possess a nuclear arsenal—is shielded from international inspection and sanctions by its policy of nuclear ambiguity. This asymmetry undermines the credibility of the NPT (Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty) regime, a grievance that G77 member states, South Africa, and Brazil have repeatedly raised in international forums.

In **2024**, South Africa's lawsuit against Israel at the International Court of Justice (ICJ) over alleged genocide was widely embraced as an expression of the Global South's collective will. Pakistan, Bangladesh, Malaysia, Turkey, Bolivia, and numerous other countries declared support for the case, calling on the international community to expand humanitarian aid to Gaza. The Global South continues to insist that resolving Middle Eastern issues requires a framework of **multilateral diplomacy** led by the region's own nations—not one dictated by the United States or Israel.
