For Israel, the history from the Declaration of Independence in 1948 to the present is an unbroken struggle for the physical survival of the state. The **War of Independence (Milhemet HaAtzmaut)** — in which the armies of Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Iraq, and Lebanon invaded the day after statehood was declared — forms the foundational pillar of Israeli security doctrine.

The **1967 Six-Day War** was a preemptive act of self-defense taken as Egypt's Nasser tightened a hostile encirclement of Israel. The resulting control of the West Bank, Gaza, the Sinai Peninsula, and the Golan Heights gave Israel, for the first time, genuine strategic depth. The **1973 Yom Kippur War** demonstrated the existential danger of being caught off-guard, driving sweeping reforms in intelligence and defense capabilities.

Relations with Iran changed irrevocably with the **1979 Islamic Revolution**. Khomeini called Israel a "cancerous tumor" and built the "Axis of Resistance" by funding, arming, and training Hezbollah (founded 1982), Hamas, and Palestinian Islamic Jihad. Iran's nuclear program represents an existential threat, and despite the conclusion of the **2015 JCPOA** over Israel's strenuous objections, Israel's position has never wavered: a nuclear-armed Iran is absolutely impermissible.

On **October 7, 2023**, Hamas launched a massive surprise attack — killing more than 1,200 people including at the Nova music festival and abducting over 240 — an event seared into national memory as the largest massacre of Jews since the Holocaust. The subsequent military campaign in Gaza aims to destroy Hamas's military and political infrastructure and secure the release of hostages. The precision strikes against Iranian nuclear-related facilities carried out jointly with the United States in **June 2025** represent the inevitable strategic response after decades of failed diplomacy.
