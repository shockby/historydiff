Japan has traditionally maintained a policy of "balanced diplomacy" in Middle Eastern affairs, supporting the two-state solution and preserving diplomatic relations with both Israel and the Palestinian Authority. Given its near-total dependence on Middle Eastern oil for energy security, successive Japanese governments have consistently avoided alignment with either the Arab or Israeli side.

Chronologically, the arc spans from the **1947 UN Partition Plan** and **Israel's 1948 independence and the First Arab-Israeli War**, through the **1967 Six-Day War**, the **1973 Yom Kippur War and the Oil Shock**, the **1979 Iranian Revolution**, the **1993 Oslo Accords**, the **2015 JCPOA**, the **October 7, 2023 Hamas surprise attack on Israel and the ensuing Gaza War**, to the **Israeli and U.S. strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in June 2025** — an unbroken chain of crises spanning eighty years.

The **1973 Oil Shock** prompted Japan to make friendly relations with Arab oil-producing states a top-priority foreign policy objective. Japan has sought to maintain a degree of engagement with Iran even under sanctions, while conducting economic and technological cooperation with Israel — yet continuing to vote in United Nations forums in support of Palestinian rights. In response to the **"Twelve-Day War" of 2025** and the subsequent question of Khomeini's succession in 2026, Japan has called for de-escalation within the G7 framework.
