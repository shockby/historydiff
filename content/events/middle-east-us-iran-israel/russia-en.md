For Russia, the Middle East serves as a critical geopolitical arena for challenging **US-led unipolar hegemony**. Foreign Minister Lavrov and President Putin have consistently framed NATO's eastward expansion and the persistent US military presence in the Middle East as expressions of "destabilizing hegemonism." In Russia's vision of a multipolar world, maintaining influence across the Middle East is an indispensable instrument for eroding American primacy in international affairs.

Russia's **military intervention in Syria beginning in 2015** marked a historic turning point in its post-Cold War Middle Eastern posture. By backing the Assad government — framed as a legitimate authority combating terrorism — Russia secured access to the Khmeimim Air Base and the Tartus naval facility, restoring a military footprint in the region it had lost after the Soviet collapse. The intervention repositioned Russia as an indispensable diplomatic actor, culminating in the **Astana Process** — a trilateral framework with Iran and Turkey that deliberately bypassed Western-led Geneva negotiations.

In its relationship with Iran, Russia has cultivated **strategic solidarity around shared adversaries**. Following the outbreak of the Ukraine war, Iran's supply of Shahed-series drones to Russia brought their defense-industrial cooperation into the open. Parallel energy cooperation and a shared posture of resisting Western sanctions further cement the partnership. That said, Moscow remains cautious about Iran acquiring nuclear weapons, framing the relationship as interest-based coordination rather than a full alliance.

On the nuclear question, Russia has consistently attacked what it calls the **West's double standard**. Moscow argues it is fundamentally unjust that Israel — a non-signatory to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty with an undeclared nuclear arsenal — faces no international pressure, while Iran is subjected to stringent inspections and crippling sanctions. Russia has repeatedly used its UN Security Council veto to block Iran sanctions resolutions, citing its commitment to "dialogue and diplomacy." This serves both as diplomatic cover for Tehran and as a mechanism to curtail American influence in the Council.

Russia **condemned Israel's strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in 2025** as "a grave violation of sovereignty and international law," a position consistent with Moscow's broader rhetorical framework of rejecting Western-backed military actions. On the Palestinian issue, Russia officially supports a two-state solution, though this backing remains largely declaratory — functioning primarily as a cudgel against US Middle East policy rather than as the basis for any substantive diplomatic initiative.
