The defining feature of the Middle East's last 80 years, as seen from the UN and international community, is the persistent gap between legal frameworks and their enforcement. The Security Council has remained functionally paralyzed, while resolutions have accumulated without effective implementation.

**UN Security Council Resolutions 242 (1967) and 338 (1973)** demanded Israeli withdrawal from occupied territories and affirmed all states' right to live within secure borders. Over half a century later, these resolutions remain unimplemented — they have become symbols of international law's impotence. The root cause lies in the **competing vetoes** within the Security Council: the United States has repeatedly vetoed resolutions critical of Israel, while Russia and China have blocked tighter sanctions on Iran.

**The IAEA** has issued repeated expressions of "serious concern" over Iran's nuclear activities since 2003. The 2015 JCPOA placed Iran's nuclear program under international oversight, but following the U.S. unilateral withdrawal in 2018, Iran curtailed cooperation with inspectors, significantly undermining the IAEA's monitoring capacity. Simultaneously, the longstanding criticism of a **"double standard on non-proliferation"** — Israel's undeclared nuclear arsenal going unchallenged despite its non-membership in the NPT — has remained a persistent grievance, especially among developing nations.

**In January 2024, the ICJ** issued provisional measures in the case brought by South Africa alleging violations of the Genocide Convention in Gaza, ordering Israel to prevent acts of genocide. This legally binding order deepened divisions between Western governments and the Global South, exposing the fragility of international consensus.

**UNRWA** has been the primary humanitarian lifeline for over two million Palestinians in Gaza. However, after Israel alleged Hamas ties among UNRWA staff, the United States and several European countries suspended funding, severely exacerbating the humanitarian crisis.

The **Israeli-U.S. military strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in June 2025** triggered an emergency Security Council session, but a condemnation resolution was vetoed by the United States. The UN General Assembly's emergency special session saw an overwhelming majority condemn the strikes as violations of the UN Charter, though its legal force remained limited. International legal scholars remain divided between interpretations of "preemptive self-defense" and "unlawful use of force."

The fundamental contradiction of the UN system — universal legal norms on paper versus great-power vetoes blocking enforcement — has nowhere been more starkly exposed than in the Middle East.
