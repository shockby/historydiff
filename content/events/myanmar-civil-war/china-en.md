China has markedly shifted its Myanmar policy from initial "strategic ambiguity" toward more overt support for the military junta. This pivot is driven by three core strategic interests: protecting the **China-Myanmar Economic Corridor (CMEC)**, stabilizing the Yunnan border, and preventing Western influence from filling a potential power vacuum. A landmark moment came in November 2024 when junta leader Min Aung Hlaing visited Kunming and met with Chinese Premier Li Qiang, granting the regime a degree of diplomatic legitimacy it had long been denied.

China has also pressured ethnic armed organizations (EAOs) to halt hostilities and enter negotiations, seeking to contain the conflict's spread. Critics argue this effectively reinforces the junta's authority and delays genuine democratic progress. Beijing views Myanmar as a vital source of critical minerals—including tin and rare-earth elements—and prioritizes "managed stability" with the junta over heeding international human rights criticism. In August 2026, Min Aung Hlaing visited Moscow to meet President Putin, further deepening the Russia-China-junta alignment.
