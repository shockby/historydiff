Myanmar's military (Tatmadaw) frames the February 2021 coup as a "constitutional and lawful transfer of power," citing alleged "large-scale election fraud" in the 2020 general elections. Commander-in-Chief Min Aung Hlaing consolidated authority at the head of the junta, eventually assuming the presidency in April 2026 through a managed "election."

The junta designates resistance forces—including ethnic armed organizations (EAOs) and People's Defense Forces (PDFs)—as "terrorists," using this framing to justify counter-insurgency operations. While widespread airstrikes and artillery bombardment of civilian infrastructure have been documented, the junta maintains that strikes target "terrorist bases." Dismissing international criticism as "interference in internal affairs," the regime has deepened diplomatic and security ties with Russia and China to break its international isolation.
