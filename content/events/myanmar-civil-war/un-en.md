The UN's response to Myanmar's coup (February 2021) and the ensuing civil war has become a defining case of the structural problem whereby the Security Council veto system prevents collective action against mass human rights violations.

**Security Council Developments:**
From immediately after the coup, Western countries sought a sanctions resolution in the Security Council, but China and Russia indicated they would use their veto or vote against it, preventing this from being realized. **S/RES/2669, adopted in December 2022**, was the first formal Security Council resolution on Myanmar (12 in favor, 3 abstentions: China, Russia, India). However, it was limited to a non-binding statement calling for a halt to violence and humanitarian access.

**UN General Assembly Developments:**
Starting with Resolution 75/287 in June 2021 (119 in favor, 1 against, 36 abstentions), the General Assembly has repeatedly adopted resolutions condemning the coup and calling for a halt to arms transfers to Myanmar. However, General Assembly resolutions have no binding legal force, and an effective arms embargo has not been achieved.

**UN Special Rapporteur on Myanmar:**
OHCHR's Special Rapporteur on Myanmar (including Tom Andrews) has continuously documented and reported military airstrikes, torture, and civilian killings, and has called for referral to the International Criminal Court (ICC). However, Myanmar is not a party to the ICC, and ICC jurisdiction cannot extend to Myanmar without a Security Council referral — which itself faces the veto wall.

This is a classic illustration of the UN system's core challenge: a large gap arises between "documented human rights violations" and "effective international sanctions" due to the permanent member veto power.
