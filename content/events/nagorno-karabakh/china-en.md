China has consistently backed Azerbaijan's position on territorial integrity in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, grounding its stance in the foundational principles of sovereignty and non-interference that underpin Chinese foreign policy. During the 2020 war, the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs declined to directly endorse Azerbaijan's military operations while repeatedly stating that "all disputes should be resolved within the framework of international law and UN Security Council resolutions" — language that implicitly aligned with Baku's claim to its internationally recognized territory. China welcomed the November 2020 Russia-brokered ceasefire as a step toward stability, carefully avoiding any formulation that could be interpreted as endorsing Armenian-Karabakh's aspirations for self-determination.

The most sensitive dimension of the Nagorno-Karabakh issue for Beijing is its intersection with the principle of self-determination. China, facing its own ethno-territorial challenges in Tibet, Xinjiang, and with respect to Taiwan, is acutely wary of any international precedent that legitimizes minority populations' claims to independence from sovereign states. Accordingly, Beijing has systematically avoided any endorsement of the Armenians of Karabakh's right to self-governance, viewing such recognition as potentially furnishing legal or moral arguments to separatist movements that China considers threats to its own territorial integrity. In September 2023, as international criticism of Azerbaijan's offensive mounted, China declined to join calls for accountability, invoking its principle of non-interference in internal affairs.

On the economic and strategic front, China has cultivated relationships with both Azerbaijan and Armenia while positioning the South Caucasus as an important corridor within the Belt and Road Initiative. Azerbaijan's energy resources and its location on the Trans-Caspian transportation route align with China's interests in diversifying overland connectivity between East Asia and Europe. China has steadily expanded bilateral economic cooperation with Baku, including infrastructure investments and growing trade. Simultaneously, Beijing values its strategic partnership with Russia, whose dominant role in South Caucasus security dynamics China is careful not to undermine. By maintaining strategic ambiguity — supporting Azerbaijan's territorial integrity without explicitly endorsing military action — China has navigated the conflict in a manner consistent with its broader foreign policy posture of advancing its interests while minimizing friction with major powers.
