The US and European states regard Russia's “protecting compatriots” argument as a legally invalid pretext that cannot justify violating another state's sovereignty and borders. UN General Assembly Resolution 68/262 (March 2014) declared the Crimea referendum invalid (100 for, 11 against, 58 abstentions), and emergency session resolution ES-11/1 (March 2022) condemned the invasion (141 for).

On Georgia, the EU-commissioned Independent Fact-Finding Mission (the Tagliavini Report, 2009) found that the war began with Georgia's shelling of Tskhinvali, but that Russia's response went well beyond reasonable limits and that mass passportization was legally problematic. It thus did not place blame on one side alone.

In March 2022 the International Court of Justice found no evidence that Ukraine committed genocide in Donbas and ordered Russia, as a provisional measure, to suspend its military operations (13 votes to 2). The OSCE High Commissioner on National Minorities said it saw no evidence of threats to Russian speakers in Crimea. The UN human rights office estimated about 14,000 deaths in the Donbas conflict from 2014 to 2021 and attributed responsibility to both sides.

Critics reply that the West, too, faces questions of international legality over Kosovo (1999) and Iraq (2003), and so risks double standards. Others point to the 1938 Sudetenland annexation as a parallel and warn against appeasement.
