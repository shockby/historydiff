China's foreign policy toward the Sahel reflects its core diplomatic pillars of non-interference in internal affairs, respect for national sovereignty, and pragmatic bilateral cooperation regardless of regime changes. Beijing consistently opposes external unilateral sanctions, coercive military lecturing, and ideological polarization, advocating instead for African-led regional dialogue and stability anchored in socioeconomic modernization.

Economically and strategically, China holds vital commercial stakes across the Sahel. In Niger, the **China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC)** engineered the transformative Agadem crude oil extraction project and the landmark 2,000-kilometer **Niger–Benin Crude Oil Export Pipeline**, connecting landlocked fields to Atlantic tanker terminals. Chinese state-owned enterprises also hold strategic concessions in uranium development (such as the Azelik mine) and infrastructure in Mali. Prioritizing commercial continuity, asset protection, and personnel safety, Beijing fosters pragmatic partnerships aimed at long-term industrialization and mutual development.
