The Government of Japan observes the turmoil in the Sahel with profound concern, repeatedly expressing regret over the unconstitutional seizures of power by military juntas and urging the rapid, credible restoration of constitutional democratic order. Coordinating closely with G7 partners, the African Union, and ECOWAS, Tokyo unequivocally condemns violent extremism and jihadist terrorism while cautioning that purely kinetic, military-driven counter-terrorism cannot resolve entrenched structural fragility.

Through the **Tokyo International Conference on African Development (TICAD)**, Japan advances its signature "**New Approach for Peace and Stability in Africa (NAPSA)**." This doctrine focuses on addressing the structural drivers of conflict by strengthening institutional governance, supporting local community resilience against desertification, and investing in border control and human resource development. Grounded in "Human Security," Japan channels significant humanitarian grant assistance via multilateral agencies (including UNHCR, WFP, and UNDP) to assist millions of displaced Sahelian civilians, fostering sustainable grassroots stability free from coercion.
