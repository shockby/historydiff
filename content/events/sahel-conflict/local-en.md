For the military governments uniting under the **Alliance of Sahel States (AES)**—Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger—the cascading putsches between 2020 and 2023 represent a profound revolution for **national sovereignty and decolonization** against French paternalism. Military leaders (Col. Assimi Goïta, Capt. Ibrahim Traoré, and Gen. Abdourahamane Tiani) contend that a decade of French military deployment (Operation Barkhane) and UN peacekeeping (MINUSMA) proved spectacularly counterproductive, failing to dismantle jihadist insurgencies while entrenching neocolonial extraction of critical raw materials like uranium and gold. Asserting their sovereign prerogative, the AES capitals expelled French and Western forces, formally withdrew from ECOWAS, and pivoted decisively toward strategic defense cooperation with **Russia** to reclaim battlefield initiative and territorial sovereignty.

Conversely, France (under President Emmanuel Macron) maintains that its 2013 Operation Serval halted an imminent extremist capture of Bamako at the direct sovereign invitation of Mali's lawful government, and that over 50 French soldiers sacrificed their lives shielding both the Sahelian populace and European security from transnational terror networks. Paris vehemently rejects accusations of neocolonial exploitation, condemning the military juntas for unconstitutionally tearing up democratic charters, scapegoating France through populist disinformation campaigns, and contracting ruthless Russian **Wagner paramilitaries (now Africa Corps)** whose scorched-earth tactics—exemplified by the 2022 Moura massacre—exacerbate ethnic polarization and fuel insurgent recruitment.
