The United Nations has navigated the deteriorating Sahelian landscape through the intersecting perils of asymmetric terrorism, constitutional collapse, and acute humanitarian breakdown. Established in 2013, the **UN Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization Mission in Mali (MINUSMA)** deployed over 15,000 personnel to protect civilian populations and support reconciliation frameworks, suffering more than 300 peacekeeper fatalities—earning the tragic designation of the UN's deadliest peacekeeping operation. In June 2023, following an ultimatum from the Malian transitional authorities, the Security Council unanimously adopted Resolution 2690 mandating the abrupt, complete departure of the mission by year-end.

In the wake of MINUSMA's drawdown, UN bodies including OHCHR and OCHA sound urgent alarms over escalating security vacuums exploited by both al-Qaeda (JNIM) and Islamic State affiliates (ISGS), alongside documented spikes in civilian casualties during joint offensives by national armies and foreign security contractors. With over **3 million internally displaced people** across the central Sahel, chronic malnutrition, and systemic school closures, the United Nations emphasizes that sustainable stability demands strict accountability under international humanitarian law, protected relief corridors, and inclusive governance architectures.
