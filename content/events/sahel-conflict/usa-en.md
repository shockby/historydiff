The United States designated the Sahel a vital frontline in counter-terrorism against transnational networks, primarily al-Qaeda affiliates (JNIM) and the Islamic State in the Greater Sahara (ISGS). For years, Washington maintained security cooperation, intelligence sharing, and major unmanned aerial reconnaissance hubs—exemplified by the $110-million **Air Base 201 in Agadez, Niger**, which anchored regional intelligence and surveillance across West Africa. However, following the July 2023 coup in Niamey and the junta's formal revocation of defense pacts, the US executed a complete, orderly withdrawal of approximately 1,000 military personnel in 2024.

Concurrently, Washington views with deep alarm the strategic inroads achieved by the Russian Federation, where Wagner paramilitaries (re-flagged as Africa Corps) provide regime protection in exchange for access to natural resources, including potential exploitation of Nigerien uranium deposits. While US statutory restrictions (Section 7008) curtailed direct security assistance to putschist regimes, Washington actively recalibrates its regional posture by bolstering the intelligence and border defenses of coastal West African democracies (Ghana, Côte d’Ivoire, Benin, Togo) while sustaining life-saving humanitarian assistance.
