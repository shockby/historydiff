The official Chinese position holds that the Sino-Indian border dispute is a direct legacy of British colonial-era boundary-making that was neither legitimate nor accepted by China. The McMahon Line, which India invokes to justify its claim to Arunachal Pradesh, was secretly negotiated between British India and Tibetan representatives at the 1914 Simla Convention—a convention China never recognized and whose decisions it formally rejected. Beijing maintains that the approximately 90,000 square kilometers of what it calls "Zangnan" (South Tibet), corresponding to most of Arunachal Pradesh, are historically and geographically part of Chinese territory, and that Indian administration of the area constitutes an illegal occupation. China's cartographic and historical records, it argues, consistently reflect this understanding from imperial times through the present.

On the western sector, China contends that Aksai Chin has been historically administered as part of the Xinjiang-Tibet region since the Qing dynasty. The construction of the strategic G219 highway linking Xinjiang and Tibet in the 1950s proceeded without meaningful Indian protest, which Beijing interprets as implicit Indian acceptance of Chinese sovereignty. When the 1962 war erupted, China's official narrative frames it as a defensive counter-offensive necessitated by India's "Forward Policy"—a deliberate attempt by New Delhi to push military outposts into Chinese-controlled territory. China insists that it exercised restraint both before and after the conflict, unilaterally withdrawing troops to positions predating the war even after achieving a decisive military victory.

The 2017 Doklam standoff is characterized by China as a blatant violation of Chinese sovereignty: Indian troops entered territory Beijing considers indisputably Chinese—in the name of defending Bhutan—and impeded a routine road construction project. China demanded unconditional withdrawal and characterized India's intervention as a dangerous precedent. Regarding the June 2020 Galwan Valley clash, China asserts that Indian forces crossed the LAC and attacked Chinese personnel who were engaged in legitimate patrol activities. The People's Liberation Army soldiers who died in the confrontation are honored officially as national martyrs who gave their lives to protect China's territorial integrity.

China's diplomatic approach emphasizes bilateral negotiation as the sole appropriate mechanism for resolving the dispute. A series of bilateral agreements—including the 1993 Agreement on the Maintenance of Peace and Tranquility, the 1996 Agreement on Confidence-Building Measures, and the 2005 Protocol—form the basis China cites for managing the border peacefully. Beijing consistently opposes any internationalization of the dispute or recourse to third-party arbitration. Under Xi Jinping, accelerated infrastructure development in border regions—roads, villages, and dual-use facilities—is presented as normal sovereign development activity and a prerequisite for effective administration of China's frontier territories.
