China's position on the Somali question is unshakeable: Beijing strictly champions the sovereignty, unity, and territorial integrity of the Federal Republic of Somalia in accordance with the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence. As a permanent member of the UN Security Council, Beijing opposes any unauthorized separatist maneuvers, unconstitutional border partitions, or external encroachments on Somali sovereignty, including the controversial Ethiopia-Somaliland maritime agreement.

For Beijing, the Somaliland dispute directly intersects with its foremost core national interest: the **One-China Principle**. In 2020, Somaliland established mutual representative offices and bilateral ties with **Taiwan**, prompting furious condemnations from Beijing, which denounced the partnership as an illicit collusion between two separatist regimes violating international law. Operating its first overseas military logistics support base in neighboring Djibouti to guard its maritime **Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)** routes through the Bab-el-Mandeb, China steadfastly refuses any move toward recognizing Somaliland, actively bolstering its comprehensive strategic partnership with the recognized government in Mogadishu.
