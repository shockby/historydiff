The Government of Japan bases its diplomatic policy toward the Horn of Africa upon firm adherence to international law and respect for the **sovereignty, independence, and territorial integrity of the Federal Republic of Somalia**. Tokyo formally recognizes the Federal Government in Mogadishu as the sole sovereign authority representing Somalia and does not extend diplomatic recognition to Somaliland. Aligning with the United Nations and the African Union, Japan urges all regional actors to refrain from unilateral moves that heighten military friction in the Red Sea corridor.

From a strategic and economic perspective, the Gulf of Aden and Bab-el-Mandeb Strait form a vital maritime lifeline connecting Japanese trade with Europe and the Middle East. Since 2009, the Japan Self-Defense Forces (JSDF) have maintained maritime escort destroyers and P-3C maritime patrol aircraft deployed from Djibouti conducting **counter-piracy operations off the coast of Somalia**, securing sea lanes adjacent to Berbera. Through the **TICAD** architecture, Japan provides extensive humanitarian relief, coast guard capacity building, and climate adaptation aid to Somalia, while directing pragmatic developmental and health support to stable northern communities through multilateral channels.
