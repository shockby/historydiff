For the Republic of Somaliland, its independence is not an illegal act of secession, but the lawful **restoration of sovereign statehood** first achieved on June 26, 1960, when the former British Somaliland protectorate gained independence and was recognized by 35 sovereign nations. Hargeisa asserts that the subsequent voluntary merger with the former Italian Trust Territory was legally flawed and ended irrevocably when the dictatorial regime of Siad Barre executed the **Isaaq Genocide** in the late 1980s, razing northern cities through aerial bombardments. While southern Somalia descended into chronic civil war, warlordism, and terrorism, Somaliland engineered a functional constitutional democracy with biometric elections, independent security forces, and a market economy for over three decades, arguing that its 2024 Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with Ethiopia rightly rewards its stability with international legitimacy.

Conversely, the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS) in Mogadishu, led by President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud, maintains that the **unity, sovereignty, and territorial integrity of Somalia** are sacred and indivisible under the Provisional Constitution. Mogadishu regards Somaliland as an integral northern federal state, dismissing its 1991 unilateral declaration of independence as a null and void rebellion. For Somalia, Ethiopia’s January 2024 MoU to lease naval base access along the Gulf of Aden in exchange for potential diplomatic recognition constitutes a flagrant breach of international law and a brazen annexation of sovereign Somali territory. Rejecting any concession of national territory, Somalia mobilized international diplomatic condemnations and forged defensive military pacts with Egypt and Turkey to defend its borders.
