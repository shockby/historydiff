The United Nations, the African Union (AU), and the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD) consistently uphold the fundamental premise that the **sovereignty, unity, and territorial integrity of the Federal Republic of Somalia** must be respected. Central to this position is the landmark 1964 OAU Cairo Resolution enshrining the inviolability of borders inherited at independence (*uti possidetis juris*). The African diplomatic consensus fears that legitimizing Somaliland’s unilateral secession would unlock a "Pandora's box," inciting ethnic separatist insurgencies and territorial fragmentation across the entire continent.

Following the January 2024 Ethiopia-Somaliland maritime agreement, the UN Security Council, the African Union, and the League of Arab States issued urgent declarations urging de-escalation and absolute adherence to international law. While international institutions steadfastly avoid granting de jure recognition that would dismantle the sovereign Somali state, they maintain substantial pragmatic relations with Hargeisa. UN developmental programs, humanitarian funding, and security partnerships quietly engage Somaliland as a functional sub-national authority, commending its sustained stability and democratic processes while reaffirming Somalia’s overarching sovereignty.
