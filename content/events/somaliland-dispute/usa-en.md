The United States officially anchors its diplomatic posture in the "**One Somalia**" policy, formally recognizing the Federal Government of Somalia in Mogadishu and repeatedly affirming the nation's territorial integrity within its 1960 borders. Following the 2024 Ethiopia-Somaliland memorandum, Washington immediately urged restraint, cautioning that diplomatic brinkmanship threatens to fracture regional security coalitions vital for prosecuting the counter-terrorism campaign against al-Qaeda affiliate al-Shabaab.

Concurrently, the US Congress, the Pentagon, and US Africa Command (AFRICOM) view Somaliland's three decades of stable democratic governance, coastal geography along the Gulf of Aden, and staunch pro-Western stance as an extraordinary strategic asset. Provisions in the US National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) mandate exploring direct security and commercial partnerships with Hargeisa. With China expanding its military presence at its strategic base in Djibouti, the US military increasingly values the expanded deep-water facilities at the **Port of Berbera** as a redundant logistics hub, pursuing a dual-track strategy that balances formal recognition of Mogadishu with deepening pragmatic engagement with Somaliland.
