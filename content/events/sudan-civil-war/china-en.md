China has adopted a posture of **"strategic ambiguity"** in the Sudan civil war, maintaining diplomatic channels with both the SAF and RSF under the banner of its "non-interference" principle. Officially, Beijing advocates three priorities: an immediate ceasefire, expanded humanitarian access, and a resumption of the political process. It supports African-led solutions through the AU and IGAD, and has consistently opposed international sanctions, arguing that such measures undermine Sudanese sovereignty.

In practice, China's position is more complex. Sudan represents a significant hub for Chinese oil and infrastructure investment, making the protection of economic interests a primary driver. In 2025, Chinese-manufactured drones and guided bombs were documented in RSF hands, prompting the Sudanese government to formally demand an explanation from Beijing. While China denies any connection to the RSF, it has avoided clear accountability. This "dual engagement" posture has drawn international criticism and raised doubts about China's credibility as an impartial mediator.
