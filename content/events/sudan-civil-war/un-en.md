The Sudan civil war (April 2023–) has become one of the most prominent cases in the 2020s of Security Council dysfunction.

**Security Council Response:**
Throughout 2023–2024, the Security Council issued multiple press statements and presidential statements, but **no binding resolution** with sanctions or enforcement measures has been adopted. The primary reason is that permanent members, particularly Russia and China, have been reluctant to support binding action due to their relationships with the Sudanese government (arms exports and diplomatic ties). S/RES/2724, adopted in 2024, is a non-binding resolution demanding humanitarian access, and its implementation has been inadequate. The deployment of a UN peacekeeping operation was also considered but was not realized, as the Sudanese government did not give its consent.

**Human Rights and Humanitarian Situation:**
A panel of UN Human Rights Council experts reported that RSF actions in Darfur may amount to "genocide." The United States formally designated these actions as genocide in 2024. As of 2024, Sudan is facing the **world's largest internal displacement crisis** (over 8 million IDPs), and UN OCHA has issued continuous emergency appeals.

**Structural Issue:**
The Security Council's response to the Sudan civil war has become an internationally noted example of the criticism that "the veto system prevents rapid international action in response to humanitarian crises," and has reinvigorated debates over Security Council reform.
