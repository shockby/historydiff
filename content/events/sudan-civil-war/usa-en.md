The United States formally determined in January 2025 that the **RSF (Rapid Support Forces)** had committed **genocide** in Darfur, imposing multiple rounds of sanctions on RSF leadership including Hemedti. In a notable escalation in July 2026, the U.S. also sanctioned the Sudanese government itself after determining that the SAF used chlorine gas in 2024—placing both warring factions under U.S. pressure simultaneously.

Diplomatically, the U.S. has worked through the **"Quad"** framework with Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE to push for ceasefires and humanitarian access. Congress introduced the bipartisan **PEACE in Sudan Act**, which could designate both factions as Specially Designated Global Terrorists to cut off their financial networks. On the humanitarian front, calls to extend **Temporary Protected Status (TPS)** for Sudanese nationals have grown as conditions worsen. Despite continued U.S. aid contributions, persistent fighting and restricted access limit the reach of these efforts.
