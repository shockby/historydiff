Japan is also a party to the history: it ruled Taiwan for 50 years, from the 1895 Treaty of Shimonoseki until its defeat in 1945. In the 1951 San Francisco Peace Treaty Japan renounced its claim to Taiwan, but the treaty does not say to whom it passed. This is one legal basis for the argument that Taiwan’s status remains undetermined.

In the 1972 Japan–China Joint Communiqué, Tokyo recognised the PRC as the sole legal government of China and stated that it “fully understands and respects” the Chinese government’s position that Taiwan is an inalienable part of PRC territory. Tokyo says this is not an endorsement of that claim; it takes no position on Taiwan’s final status and calls for a “peaceful resolution”. It maintains unofficial working relations with Taiwan through the Japan–Taiwan Exchange Association.

Peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait are directly tied to Japan’s security. Yonaguni Island lies about 110 km from Taiwan, and Japan’s sea lanes pass around the island. Former Prime Minister Abe said in 2021 that “a Taiwan contingency is a Japan contingency”, and in November 2025 Prime Minister Takaichi told the Diet that a Taiwan contingency could constitute a “survival-threatening situation”, drawing a strong Chinese reaction. Within Japan, opinion is divided on how to balance stable relations with China against support for Taiwan and alliance cooperation.
