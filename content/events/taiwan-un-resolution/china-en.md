The official position of the People's Republic of China (PRC) is that Taiwan is an inalienable part of Chinese territory and that the Taiwan issue is an internal affair of China.

According to Chinese textbooks and government positions, UN General Assembly Resolution 2758 of 1971 was a historic decision in which the international community formally recognized this position. The resolution recognized that "the representatives of the Government of the People's Republic of China are the only lawful representatives of China to the United Nations" and expelled the representatives of the Republic of China (Taiwan) from the UN. China interprets this resolution as having established the "One China Principle" and as having confirmed under international law that Taiwan is part of China.

In China's historical education, Japan's defeat in 1945 returned Taiwan to the "motherland," and the Republic of China government that has maintained effective control over Taiwan since the end of the civil war in 1949 is not the legitimate government of China. Current moves toward "separatist independence" in Taiwan are regarded as a serious challenge to China's core interests, and the stance of "not renouncing the use of force" is enshrined in the Constitution (Anti-Secession Law). Diplomatic relations, military cooperation, or arms sales to Taiwan that do not accept the "One China" principle are subject to diplomatic protest.
