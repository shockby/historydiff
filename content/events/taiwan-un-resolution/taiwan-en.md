Taiwan's (Republic of China's) position is that Resolution 2758 did not directly determine "Taiwan's status" and did not internationally recognize the PRC's claim that "Taiwan is part of the People's Republic of China."

According to the views of Taiwan's government and academic institutions, Resolution 2758 decided at the 1971 UN General Assembly to transfer the "China" seat from the Republic of China (its official name at the time) to the People's Republic of China. However, a careful reading of the resolution text reveals that it says nothing about "Taiwan's attribution" or "the political status of Taiwan's inhabitants." The resolution text nowhere states that Taiwan is "a province of the People's Republic of China," and Taiwan counters that the PRC government's claim that "Resolution 2758 established the One China Principle" goes beyond the intent of the resolution text.

Taiwan is a democratic self-governing entity of over 23 million people with its own government, military, currency, and passports. Current public opinion surveys show that a majority of residents identify as "Taiwanese" and prioritize the defense of Taiwan's democracy. Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs seeks opportunities to participate in international organizations (particularly WHO, ICAO, INTERPOL, etc.), which connects to the issue that "23 million people in Taiwan are excluded from international decision-making." In Taiwan's academic and civil society circles, there is a growing international movement calling for a "reinterpretation" of Resolution 2758.
