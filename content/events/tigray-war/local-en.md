For the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia led by Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, the military campaign launched in November 2020 was a constitutionally mandated "**Law Enforcement Operation**" aimed at preserving the sovereign integrity of the state against an insurrection by the **Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF)**. Addis Ababa asserted that the TPLF—which had monopolized authoritarian federal power for 27 years through the EPRDF coalition until 2018—refused to accept democratic reforms, conducted an unconstitutional regional election in September 2020, and crossed a definitive "red line" on the night of November 3 by launching a coordinated, treasonous assault on federal Northern Command bases to seize heavy weaponry. The federal government maintained that neutralizing rogue regional militarism was vital to safeguarding national unity and civic order.

Conversely, the TPLF and Tigrayan regional leadership framed the war as an existential war of defense against coordinated state aggression and ethnic annihilation. The TPLF argued that Prime Minister Abiy sought to dismantle the 1995 Constitution's cornerstone of "**ethnic federalism**"—which guarantees self-determination and autonomy to historical nationalities—in order to impose a centralized autocratic hegemony under his Prosperity Party. Tigrayan authorities vociferously condemned Addis Ababa for inviting foreign troops from **Eritrea** and neighboring Amhara regional forces to commit atrocities, including mass killings in Axum, widespread weaponized rape, and a suffocating blockade of fuel, banking, electricity, and food aid intended to starve the region into submission. While the November 2022 **Pretoria Agreement** achieved a cessation of hostilities, deep disputes regarding territorial borders, rehabilitation, and the complete withdrawal of Eritrean troops endure.
