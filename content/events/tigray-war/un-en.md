The United Nations and the African Union (AU) operated under the dual imperatives of halting catastrophic violations of international humanitarian law and facilitating an African-led diplomatic resolution. A landmark joint investigation conducted by the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) and the Ethiopian Human Rights Commission concluded that all warring parties—including Ethiopian federal forces, the Eritrean Defence Forces, TPLF combatants, and regional militias—committed gross abuses, including indiscriminate civilian shelling, extrajudicial executions, widespread sexual violence, and forced displacements, which may constitute **war crimes and crimes against humanity**.

The UN humanitarian leadership persistently condemned the protracted operational siege of Tigray, demanding immediate, unfettered humanitarian corridors to avert widespread starvation and disease among millions of stranded civilians. Operating under the guiding doctrine of "African solutions to African problems," the African Union appointed former Nigerian President Olusegun Obasanjo as High Representative to mediate direct negotiations. These efforts culminated in the historic signing of the **Pretoria Agreement** on November 2, 2022, in South Africa. The UN Security Council endorsed the cessation of hostilities, stressing that lasting peace requires rigorous monitoring of disarmament, complete foreign troop withdrawal, sustained humanitarian rehabilitation, and comprehensive transitional justice.
