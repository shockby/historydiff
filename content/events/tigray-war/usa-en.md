The United States, historically a premier security and development partner of Ethiopia, exercised substantial diplomatic pressure and financial sanctions in response to the escalating humanitarian crisis and reported atrocities in Tigray. In September 2021, President Joe Biden issued Executive Order 14046 establishing a robust sanctions architecture targeting entities perpetuating the conflict, followed by the punitive termination of Ethiopia’s duty-free trade eligibility under the **African Growth and Opportunity Act (AGOA)** citing gross violations of internationally recognized human rights.

Washington forcefully demanded the immediate lifting of the blockade restricting humanitarian access, electricity, and telecommunications to Tigray, while repeatedly calling for the full, verifiable withdrawal of **Eritrean forces**. Simultaneously, the US Special Envoy for the Horn of Africa engaged in rigorous shuttle diplomacy, partnering closely with the African Union mediation team to facilitate the breakthrough negotiations that yielded the Pretoria Agreement. Following the ceasefire, the United States conditioned the full normalization of bilateral ties on the sustained implementation of the accord, unhindered aid delivery, and credible transitional justice mechanisms for war victims.
