China has never recognized Transnistria (the Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic, PMR) and nominally supports Moldova's territorial integrity. In practice, however, Beijing maintains an extremely low profile on the Transnistrian conflict, refraining from any criticism of Russia's role and invoking its principle of non-interference to justify limited engagement. This posture is consistent with its broader strategic partnership with Moscow. As with South Ossetia, Abkhazia, and the TRNC, recognizing any breakaway territory would directly contradict China's own positions on Taiwan, Tibet, and Xinjiang — making PMR recognition categorically off the table.

Economically, China-Moldova bilateral trade relations have expanded modestly in recent years. Moldova occupies a marginal position in China's Belt and Road Initiative, with limited Chinese investment focused primarily on agricultural and infrastructure cooperation. Transnistria itself has virtually no direct economic relationship with China, given its limited external economic ties and dependence on Russian structures. Since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, China has avoided issuing official statements on Moldova's EU integration trajectory, seeking to maintain a low profile that avoids directly positioning Beijing against either Chisinau or Moscow.

China's primary concern regarding the Transnistrian conflict is the Western strategy of using frozen conflicts as levers of pressure on Russia. Beijing views with skepticism any effort to deploy the Transnistrian issue within the framework of sanctions on Russia or broader anti-Russian diplomatic pressure. China has implicitly accepted the presence of Russian military forces in Transnistria as a legitimate security concern for Moscow. Chinese state media, covering the 2024–2025 Transnistrian energy crisis triggered by Ukraine's halt of Russian gas transit, has in some instances framed Ukraine's gas transit cutoff as a destabilizing action, reflecting Beijing's consistent narrative of attributing regional tensions in part to Western and Ukrainian policy choices.
