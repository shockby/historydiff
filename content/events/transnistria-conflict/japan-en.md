Japan maintains a consistent position in support of Moldova's sovereignty and territorial integrity, and views the Russian military presence in Transnistria as a destabilizing factor in breach of international norms. The Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs characterizes Moldova as a partner committed to democratic reform and European integration, extending full political backing to Chișinău's EU accession candidacy. Japan voted in favor of UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/72/282 (2018) calling for the unconditional withdrawal of Russian forces from Moldovan territory, aligning itself with the Western-led consensus on the illegality of the OGRF presence. Bilateral relations between Japan and Moldova are modest in scale but substantive: Japanese Official Development Assistance (ODA) has supported Moldova's judicial reform, public administration capacity building, and civil society development over the past two decades.

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 elevated Japan's engagement with European security issues, including the Transnistrian dimension. As a G7 member, Japan joined successive rounds of sanctions against Russia and provided significant financial and humanitarian assistance to Ukraine. Japanese government statements have explicitly expressed concern about potential spillover effects from the Ukraine war into Moldova via Transnistria, and Japan has contributed funding to programs supporting Ukrainian refugee reception in Moldova and food security assistance. Japanese policymakers have drawn implicit parallels between Russia's use of frozen conflicts as instruments of geopolitical leverage and Japan's own experience with unresolved territorial disputes, lending Tokyo a degree of structural sympathy for Chișinău's predicament.

Japan's broader foreign policy framework under successive administrations—most notably the Kishida government (2021–2024)—has emphasized the defense of a "rules-based international order" and the inadmissibility of changing borders by force. This principle has been applied consistently to Eastern Europe, the South Caucasus, and East Asia alike, and Japanese officials have drawn explicit linkages between Russian behavior in Ukraine and Transnistria and potential challenges to the post-1945 territorial order in the Indo-Pacific. Japan's strengthened cooperation with NATO and European partners since 2022 has created institutional channels through which concerns about Moldova's stability can be coordinated multilaterally. In the context of the 2024 Transnistrian energy crisis and the PMR parliament's appeal to Russia for "protection," Japan joined G7 partners in reaffirming solidarity with Moldova's sovereignty.

Within Japan's academic and media landscape, awareness of the Transnistria conflict has grown since 2022, driven primarily by reporting on the Ukraine war. Security analysts have examined the risk that Russian forces in Transnistria could be activated to open a second front against Ukraine's southern flank, and have assessed Moldova's institutional vulnerabilities in the face of hybrid warfare. Japan's preferred approach remains a multilateral one—supporting OSCE-led negotiations, contributing to EU stabilization efforts, and sustaining bilateral ODA to Moldova—rather than direct bilateral diplomatic intervention. Japanese public opinion, while broadly supportive of Ukraine and critical of Russia's revisionist foreign policy, has limited awareness of Transnistria as a distinct issue, but the Ukraine war context has meaningfully elevated policymaker attention to the region's fragility.
