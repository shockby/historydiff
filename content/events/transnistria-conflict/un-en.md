The United Nations has consistently affirmed the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Republic of Moldova. UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/72/282, adopted in June 2018 with support from over 100 member states, called for "the complete and unconditional withdrawal of foreign military forces from the territory of the Republic of Moldova"—a direct reference to Russia's Operational Group of Russian Forces (OGRF) stationed in Transnistria. The resolution was opposed by Russia, Belarus, and China, reflecting broader geopolitical fault lines. Within the UN system, the Transnistria situation is categorized as a "frozen conflict"—one where open hostilities have ceased but no comprehensive political settlement has been achieved, and where an external military presence sustains the status quo. UN agencies, including UNDP, operate within Transnistria on development and civil society projects without conferring any recognition on the PMR's claimed statehood.

The Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) has served as the primary multilateral mediator since establishing its Mission to Moldova in 1993. The OSCE leads the "5+2" negotiation format, which brings together Moldova and Transnistria as the two parties, Russia and Ukraine as guarantors, the OSCE itself as mediator, and the European Union and the United States as observers. The format aims to achieve a "comprehensive, exclusive and binding settlement" of the Transnistrian conflict, defined as a special status for Transnistria within a sovereign and territorially intact Moldovan state. Incremental confidence-building measures—covering customs, education, telecommunications, and freedom of movement—have been negotiated, but fundamental issues of political status remain unresolved. The OSCE has also consistently demanded the withdrawal of Russian troops and the disposal of ammunition stockpiles held at Cobasna, near the Ukrainian border, which contain an estimated 20,000 tonnes of Soviet-era munitions.

The European Union views the Transnistria conflict through the dual lens of its Eastern Partnership policy and the broader security architecture of the European continent. Following the EU–Moldova Association Agreement of 2014 and, crucially, Moldova's award of EU candidate status in June 2022, the EU has intensified its engagement with Chișinău on reform, rule of law, and border security. European Parliament resolutions have consistently characterized the OGRF presence as a violation of international law and have called for its withdrawal. The ECHR's landmark ruling in Ilaşcu and Others v. Moldova and Russia (Application No. 48787/99, 2004) found that Russia exercises "effective control" over the PMR and bears responsibility for human rights violations committed there. The EU has also pursued "small steps" practical engagement with Transnistria—extending the EU's Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area (DCFTA) benefits, facilitating Moldovan and Romanian passport acquisition for PMR residents—as instruments of gradual, non-coercive integration.

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 severely disrupted the 5+2 framework. With Russia transformed from a putative mediator into a party to an adjacent armed conflict, and Ukraine withdrawing practical cooperation from the format, multilateral diplomacy on Transnistria has been effectively paralyzed. The OSCE's internal consensus requirements make it difficult to take strong collective positions. Moldova has negotiated with EU partners the principle that EU accession candidacy will not be formally blocked by the unresolved Transnistrian question—a pragmatic accommodation, but one that leaves the long-term status issue deferred. International consensus remains firm that no state recognizes PMR independence; even Russia, despite its deep political, economic, and military involvement, has not extended formal diplomatic recognition. The 2024 energy crisis, triggered by the cessation of Russian gas transit through Ukraine, further underlined the PMR's structural vulnerability and the urgency of a durable resolution.
