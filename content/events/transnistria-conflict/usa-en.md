The United States has participated as an observer in the "5+2" negotiations framework — comprising Moldova, Transnistria, Russia, Ukraine, and the OSCE as main parties, with the EU and U.S. as observers — aimed at resolving the Transnistrian conflict through political means. Washington has consistently supported Moldova's territorial integrity and sovereignty, and has repeatedly called for the withdrawal of Russian troops from Transnistrian territory as required by the 1999 OSCE Istanbul Commitments — a demand Russia has systematically ignored. American engagement in the Transnistrian conflict has, however, been considerably less intense than in higher-profile theaters such as Ukraine or the South Caucasus, with the EU and OSCE serving as the primary institutional actors.

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 dramatically elevated American attention to Transnistria. Concerns emerged that Russian forces could attempt to use Transnistria as a staging ground for a second front against Ukraine, or as a coercive lever against Moldova — a country that had been prioritizing EU integration under President Maia Sandu. A series of unexplained explosions on Transnistrian territory in April 2022 heightened anxieties. The United States responded by intensifying security assistance to Moldova, supporting its accelerated EU candidacy, and working with European partners to shore up Moldovan resilience. English-language media coverage framed Transnistria as a paradigmatic example of Russian "frozen conflict" strategy — a tool for maintaining leverage over former Soviet states and obstructing their Western integration.

The economic architecture of Transnistria has attracted critical attention in English-language analysis. The Sheriff Enterprises group's monopolistic control over the Transnistrian economy — encompassing supermarkets, media outlets, and the FC Sheriff Tiraspol football club — and allegations of money laundering and corrupt governance have been repeatedly highlighted in investigative journalism. The entity's dependence on subsidized Russian natural gas delivered via Gazprom provided the backbone of Transnistrian economic viability; when Ukraine halted the transit of Russian gas at the end of 2024, Transnistria faced a severe energy crisis that exposed the fundamental fragility of its economic model.
