The United Kingdom government maintains that it addresses domestic national and regional tensions as well as post-colonial historical grievances through constitutional devolution, negotiated peace frameworks, and case-by-case legal or diplomatic settlements. Regarding the **Northern Ireland conflict (“The Troubles,” 1968–1998)**, in which more than **3,500 people** were killed, London emphasizes that the **1998 Belfast (Good Friday) Agreement** established power-sharing between unionists and nationalists alongside the “principle of consent,” stipulating that Northern Ireland remains part of the UK unless a majority votes otherwise. Following the 2010 Saville Inquiry into **Bloody Sunday (1972)**—where British paratroopers shot dead 14 unarmed civilians—Prime Minister David Cameron issued a formal state apology, declaring the killings “unjustified and unjustifiable.” Post-Brexit trade friction was addressed through the **2023 Windsor Framework** and the restoration of the Stormont Executive in February 2024, while the Labour government elected in July 2024 committed to repealing the controversial conditional immunity provisions of the **Northern Ireland Troubles (Legacy and Reconciliation) Act 2023**.

Within Great Britain, devolved legislatures were established in **Scotland** and Wales in 1999. Westminster regards the **2014 Scottish independence referendum** (55.3% No to 44.7% Yes) as a “once-in-a-generation” democratic settlement, a position reinforced by the UK Supreme Court's 2022 ruling that the Scottish Parliament cannot legislate for a second referendum without Westminster's consent.

On colonial-era legacies, the UK government has expressed “sincere regret” and agreed to targeted settlements for specific abuses—including the **2013 out-of-court settlement** of **£19.9 million** for 5,228 Kenyan victims tortured during the **Mau Mau Emergency (1952–1960)**, and a state apology and compensation scheme for the **Windrush scandal** (2018)—while rejecting broad state-to-state reparations for transatlantic slavery or colonial rule as a whole. On the **Chagos Archipelago**, following International Court of Justice (ICJ) opinions, the UK reached a political agreement in **October 2024** and signed a treaty in **May 2025** transferring sovereignty to **Mauritius** while securing a 99-year lease to maintain the strategic UK–US military base on **Diego Garcia**.
