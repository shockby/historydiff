China has maintained a posture of "strategic neutrality" regarding **Russia's invasion of Ukraine**, consistently avoiding a clear alignment with either side. The Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs refers to the conflict as the "Ukraine crisis" and has steadfastly refused to characterize Russia's actions as "aggression."

The official Chinese position holds that **NATO's eastward expansion** is the structural root cause of the crisis, and that the "Cold War mentality" of the United States and Western nations has destabilized the region. At the same time, Beijing formally affirms its respect for the territorial integrity of all sovereign states and the principle of non-interference in internal affairs — a combination it uses to maintain its distinctive diplomatic posture.

In **February 2023**, China released a 12-point document titled "China's Position on the Political Settlement of the Ukraine Crisis," calling for a ceasefire and resolution through dialogue. However, the document contained no specific demands for Russian withdrawal or the return of occupied territories, and was criticized by Western governments as effectively endorsing Russia's invasion. Ukrainian President Zelensky expressed cautious appreciation for China's potential mediating role while pressing for more concrete action.

Economically, China has not joined Western sanctions against Russia; Sino-Russian trade expanded substantially after the invasion. The sharp increase in energy imports — particularly oil and natural gas purchased from Russia — has partially offset the impact of Western sanctions. China also abstained in the UN General Assembly vote on the **September 2022 Russian annexation declaration** affecting four Ukrainian oblasts.
