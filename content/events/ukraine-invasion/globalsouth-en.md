Many countries of the **Global South** — including India, Brazil, South Africa, Turkey, and numerous African nations — have taken positions on Russia's invasion of Ukraine that diverge markedly from those of the Western bloc. These countries have declined to join sanctions against Russia, prioritizing what they call "strategic autonomy" in foreign policy.

**India** is the most prominent example. It dramatically increased its crude oil imports from Russia after the invasion. Prime Minister Modi declared "this is not an era of war," yet India has consistently abstained in UN General Assembly votes condemning Russia's actions. India's long-standing military and diplomatic ties with Russia — rooted in Soviet-era weapons supply relationships — underpin its carefully maintained non-alignment position.

**Brazil** under President Lula (from 2023) has actively sought a mediating role, framing the conflict as one in which "both sides bear responsibility" and positioning itself as a neutral peace broker. Lula has proposed a "peace club" of countries that are neither belligerents nor sanctions participants to lead the peace process.

**Africa** has felt the war's effects acutely through soaring **food and fertilizer prices**. The collapse of the **Black Sea Grain Initiative** in 2022 disrupted wheat and sunflower oil imports, worsening food security crises across the continent. In 2023, leaders from six African nations — as part of the "African Peace Mission" under the AU chairmanship of Comoros — visited both Zelensky and Putin in an independent mediation effort.

A sentiment widely shared across the Global South is that "this is Europe's war," and that it is diverting resources from their more pressing priorities: poverty, climate change, and debt crises.
