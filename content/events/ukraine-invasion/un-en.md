Russia's invasion of Ukraine (February 24, 2022) sharply exposed a structural problem in the United Nations: the paralysis of the Security Council and the substitution of action by the General Assembly.

**Veto in the Security Council:**
On February 25, 2022, the Security Council voted on a draft resolution demanding Russia's immediate withdrawal, but Russia exercised its veto to block it (11 in favor, 1 against, 3 abstentions: China, India, UAE). The structural flaw of the UN Charter's Security Council system — that permanent members can exercise their veto even when they are a party to the dispute — was once again exposed.

**Emergency Special Session (11th) Resolutions (no binding force; political statements):**
Convened under the "Uniting for Peace" resolution, the Emergency Special Session adopted the following key resolutions from 2022 onward:
- **ES-11/1 (March 2, 2022)**: Demanded Russia's immediate ceasefire and full troop withdrawal (141 in favor, 5 against, 35 abstentions). Only Russia, Belarus, North Korea, Eritrea, and Syria voted against.
- **ES-11/3 (April 7, 2022)**: Suspended Russia from the UN Human Rights Council (93 in favor, 24 against, 58 abstentions).
- **ES-11/4 (October 12, 2022)**: Declared Russia's "annexation" of 4 oblasts unlawful (143 in favor, 5 against, 35 abstentions).
- **ES-11/6 (February 23, 2023)**: On the first anniversary of the invasion, again demanded Russia's withdrawal (141 in favor, 7 against, 32 abstentions).

**International Court of Justice (ICJ) Provisional Measures:**
On March 16, 2022, the ICJ issued provisional measures at Ukraine's request, ordering Russia to "immediately suspend the military operations that it commenced on 24 February 2022" (13 in favor, 2 against). Russia denied the ICJ's jurisdiction and ignored the order, continuing military operations.

These responses collectively represent a landmark case of the "will of the supermajority vs. institutional limits" — the General Assembly succeeded in isolating Russia with overwhelming support, while not a single binding Security Council sanction was ever enacted.
