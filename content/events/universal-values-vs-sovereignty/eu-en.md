The EU's treaties (**Articles 2 and 21 TEU**) name democracy, the rule of law and human rights as its foundation and as guiding principles of its external action. Human-rights clauses in trade and cooperation agreements ("**conditionality**") and the **EU Global Human Rights Sanctions Regime** (the so-called EU Magnitsky Act, adopted in 2020) are signature tools of values-based diplomacy. After Russia's invasion of Ukraine the EU further expanded sanctions justified on rule-of-law and human-rights grounds.

Internal contradictions undercut the message. Disputes over judicial independence and media freedom in **Hungary and Poland** raised the question of whether the EU can lecture others. Deals with Turkey and North African states on migration, and the 2011 Libya intervention, are cited as examples of interests outweighing principle. To governments invoking non-interference, the EU responds that **by joining the UN Charter and human-rights treaties, states made human rights a matter of legitimate international concern, not purely a domestic one**.
