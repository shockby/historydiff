As the world's largest democracy, India does not reject democratic values: Prime Minister Modi calls India the "**mother of democracy**," and it has taken part in the Summit for Democracy. Yet it is **wary of external pressure or sanctions over human rights and governance**, and under "**strategic autonomy**" it joins neither the Western nor the Russia–China camp. After Russia invaded Ukraine, India abstained on most UN condemnation votes and kept importing Russian oil.

Many Global South states (emerging and developing countries in Asia, Africa and Latin America) share a historical distrust that "**intervention in the name of human rights is selective and has masked colonial and resource interests**." Indonesia, Brazil, South Africa and others likewise tend to **prioritize development and economic cooperation over values-based blocs**. India itself faces domestic and international criticism over press freedom and minority rights, and the government's insistence that these are internal matters causes friction with international NGOs and the US Congress.
