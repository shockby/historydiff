As a G7 member, Japan makes cooperation with countries that share "**universal values** such as freedom, democracy, fundamental human rights and the rule of law" a pillar of its diplomacy, and it has framed its **"Free and Open Indo-Pacific"** vision in these terms. After Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine it joined G7 sanctions and called for defending the international order.

Tokyo's approach nevertheless differs from Washington's and Brussels'. On **rights issues in China, Myanmar and elsewhere** it favors "dialogue and engagement" over strong sanctions, and a **Japanese Magnitsky-style law** allowing targeted sanctions has been debated in the Diet but not enacted. Contributing factors include deep economic ties, the wish to avoid worsening relations with Asian neighbors, and a postwar diplomatic tradition that values non-interference. Japan is often described as occupying a **middle position, balancing universal values against respect for national circumstances**.
