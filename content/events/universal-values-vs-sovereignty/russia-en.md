Russia reads Georgia's 2003 Rose Revolution, Ukraine's 2004 Orange Revolution, Kyrgyzstan's 2005 Tulip Revolution and Ukraine's 2014 upheaval as **"color revolutions" backed by Western funding and NGOs**. President Putin and Chief of General Staff Gerasimov have described this as a new form of warfare and a national-security threat. That perception underpins the **"foreign agents" law (since 2012)** and the "undesirable organizations" designation used to restrict foreign-funded NGOs and media.

Moscow promotes the idea of "**sovereign democracy**," arguing each state decides its own political form, and repeatedly cites **Article 2(7) of the UN Charter** on non-intervention. It points to the Iraq War and the Libya intervention, where **Western states used force without clear UN Security Council authorization**, as proof of double standards. Ukraine, European governments and rights groups respond that Russia's curbs on elections and the press, its repression of dissent, and above all **its 2022 invasion of Ukraine, a violation of sovereignty,** make its non-interference argument unconvincing.
