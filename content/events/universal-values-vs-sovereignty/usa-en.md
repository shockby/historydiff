Building on the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)**, the United States has long framed freedom of expression, fair elections, the rule of law and human rights as "**universal values**" and a pillar of its foreign policy. After the Cold War, democracy promotion was tied to security through the "democratic peace" idea that democracies rarely fight one another. In 2021 Washington hosted the **Summit for Democracy**, casting global competition as "democracy versus autocracy." Support for civil society and elections through bodies such as the National Endowment for Democracy (NED) and USAID was a concrete tool of this approach.

The US record draws criticism at home and abroad: the 2003 Iraq War, partly justified in terms of democratization, produced chaos; allies with poor rights records are often treated leniently, which critics call a double standard; and from **2025 the Trump administration sharply cut USAID and scaled back democracy and human rights assistance**. China and Russia describe US democracy aid as a vehicle for "color revolutions." Washington replies that such support helps citizens exercise their own choices and is not regime-change engineering.
