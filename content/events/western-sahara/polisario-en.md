The position of the Polisario Front and the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR) is that Western Sahara is a "Non-Self-Governing Territory" within the UN decolonization framework, and that the Sahrawi people have the right to a referendum on independence based on the principle of self-determination.

The Polisario argues that Spain's 1975 withdrawal without proper transitional measures for the Sahrawi people constituted "incomplete decolonization." The International Court of Justice's 1975 advisory opinion stated that Western Sahara "was not a territory belonging to no one (terra nullius)" at the time of Spanish colonization, while also finding that "no tie of territorial sovereignty" between Morocco or Mauritania and Western Sahara had been established. The United Nations has interpreted this as "confirming the right to self-determination of the Sahrawi people." The Polisario accepted the 1991 ceasefire with Morocco and cooperated with UN MINURSO (Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara), but criticizes Morocco for effectively blocking the referendum for over 30 years.

Approximately 170,000 Sahrawi refugees live in camps in Tindouf, Algeria, with the prolonged refugee situation constituting a serious humanitarian problem. The SADR is a member of the African Union (AU) and is recognized as a state by approximately 40 countries.
