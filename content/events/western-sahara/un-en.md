The United Nations continues to classify Western Sahara as a "Non-Self-Governing Territory" subject to decolonization. This is fundamentally incompatible with Morocco's claim of sovereignty over the region as its "Southern Provinces," and the UN's consistent official position is to treat the issue as an "unresolved decolonization question."

**1975 ICJ Advisory Opinion (the core point of divergence):**
The ICJ found that "Western Sahara was not a territory belonging to no one (terra nullius) at the time of colonization by Spain" but also that "no tie of territorial sovereignty" between Morocco (or Mauritania) and Western Sahara had been established. Morocco interprets this as "international confirmation of historical ties," while the UN and Polisario interpret it as "confirmation of ties without sovereignty, hence confirming the right to self-determination." The same document is cited in diametrically opposite ways — a classic example of an interpretive "diff."

**MINURSO (UN Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara):**
Established by UN Security Council Resolution 690 in 1991, with a mandate to monitor the ceasefire and organize a referendum. The ceasefire has held, but the referendum has **not been held for over 30 years**, because Morocco demands independence be excluded as an option and negotiations over voter eligibility collapsed. Annual Security Council resolutions extend MINURSO's mandate, but no referendum date has been set. This is one of the longest-standing cases of a UN mission unable to achieve its mandate.

**The 2020 U.S. Policy Shift:**
In December 2020, the Trump administration became the first U.S. government to recognize Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara, in exchange for Morocco normalizing relations with Israel (Abraham Accords). The Biden administration did not formally reverse this recognition. This position is incompatible with the UN's official stance (Non-Self-Governing Territory) and also runs counter to Security Council consensus.
